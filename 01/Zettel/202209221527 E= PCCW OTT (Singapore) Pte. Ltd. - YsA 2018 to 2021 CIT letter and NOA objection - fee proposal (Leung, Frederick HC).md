Writing-Down Allowances for Intellectual Property Rights (IPRs)

For the purpose of claiming Section 19B writing-down allowances, IPRs are:

a.	Patents
b.	Copyrights
c.	Trademarks
d.	Registered designs
e.	Geographical indications
f.	Lay-out designs of integrated circuit
g.	Trade secrets or information with commercial value*
h.	Plant varieties

* In line with the policy intent of Section 19B of the Income Tax Act 1947, in the definition of IPR, the expressions 'trade secret' and 'information that has commercial value', and any work or subject-matter to which the expression 'copyright' relates, exclude the following:

1.	Information of customers of a trade or business, such as a list of those customers and requirements of those customers, gathered in the course of carrying on that trade or business
2.	Information on work processes (such as standard operating procedures), other than industrial information, or technique, that is likely to assist in the manufacture or processing of goods or materials
3.	Compilation of any information as described in paragraph (1) or (2); and such other matter as the Minister may by regulations prescribe

To be eligible for writing-down allowances, the transferee (i.e. company that acquires the IPRs) must acquire the legal and economic ownership of the IPRs from the transferor (i.e. person who sells the IPRs to the transferee), except for cases where approval for waiver of the requirement for legal ownership has been granted by the Singapore Economic Development Board (EDB) under Section 19B(2B) of the Income Tax Act 1947.

Legal ownership means that the legal rights to the IPRs are granted to the transferee.

Economic ownership means that the future economic benefits arising from the IPRs will accrue to the transferee.

For the purpose of claiming writing-down allowances, capital expenditure excludes legal fees, registration fees, stamp duty and other costs related to the acquisition of the IPRs.

Please refer to the following websites for further information:
- [IRAS | Writing-Down Allowances for Intellectual Property Rights (IPRs)](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/corporate-income-tax/income-deductions-for-companies/claiming-allowances/writing-down-allowances-for-intellectual-property-rights-(iprs))
- [Intellectual Property Office of Singapore (IPOS)](https://www.ipos.gov.sg/)

a.	Details of IPR acquired
i.	Brief description of IPR acquired
ii.	Name and address of the transferor (person from whom the IPR is acquired)
iii.	Relationship with the transferor
iv.	Capital expenditure incurred in acquiring the IPR
v.	Date on which the capital expenditure is incurred
b.	A copy of the sales and purchase agreements between the company and the transferor for the acquisition of the IPR
c.	A copy of third party independent valuation report if:
o	the capital expenditure incurred in acquiring the IPR is equal to or greater than S$0.5 million for a related party transaction; or
o	the capital expenditure incurred in acquiring the IPR is equal to or greater than S$2 million for an unrelated party transaction.
d.	Confirmation of the following:
o	The IPR is acquired by the Company for use in its trade or business
o	The Company has both the legal and economic ownership of the IPR acquired
e.	Whether the Company will like to claim the writing down allowances over a period of 5 / 10 / 15 years.
