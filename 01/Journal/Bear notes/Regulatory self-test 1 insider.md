# Regulatory self-test 1: insider
A requirement of the Model Code of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange is that directors must not deal in shares of their company when they are in possession of inside information. In addition they must not deal in the company’s shares in the period 60 days before publication of its annual results or in the period 30 days before publication of its quarterly results (if any) and half-year results.

Required

Why is this rule considered necessary?
- - - -
## Solutions
The Rule is partly a statement of the law, that insider dealing is illegal. If a director has inside information about the company, he or she must not deal in the company’s shares. This is a legal requirement.

The aim is to **minimise crime and misconduct** in the markets. The 60-day rule and 30-day rule provide ~reassurance to investors~. This is because it can be ::difficult to prove insider dealing in a court of law::. For example, if a director sells shares in the company 25 days before publication of the annual results, could this be because the director knows that the results will be disappointing and the share price will fall when the results are announced, or not? It could be difficult to prove insider dealing, but the Model Code removes the doubt and concern by stating that the director cannot deal in the shares (except in exceptional circumstances).
