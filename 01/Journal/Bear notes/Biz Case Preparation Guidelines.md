# Biz Case Preparation Guidelines
## Executive Summary
The purpose of the Executive Summary is to provide a structured summary of the nature of an investment opportunity together with the various options and their associated risks and projected returns, which will enable Management to assess whether or not the proposal is of the best interest to the Group. The Executive Summary will also outline implementation plans, key milestones and proposed accountabilities that can then be used as the basis for subsequent monitoring, control and BENEFIT REALIZATION. Justifications for the expenditure, benefits in terms of revenue growth, revenue protection, cost savings, support demand should be highlighted as appropriate.

    * Who is the project sponsor and project manager?
    * Project Implementation Plan - project plan, project approach & implementation (decision/actions required/key milestones), project    team composition with respective roles & responsibilities (especially cross-BU    initiatives). 
   
### Investment and Return
    * Financial Justification (IRR, NPV, payback, marketing plans… etc).
    * Sensitivity analysis on key attributes.
    * Forecast of Cashflow profile. 

### Benefit Tracking (KPI & Milestones)
    * Key deliverables and milestones.
    * Quantifiable benefits.
    * 8 quarters of KPIs are required for CAPEX project benefit tracking. 
	    * For applications > HK$10M, minimum 3 sets of KPIs are required.
	    * For applications > HK$2M, minimum 1 set of KPI is required.   
	    * For other applications, KPIs are strongly recommended to facilitate proper benefit tracking. 
### CAPEX / Investment Fund Breakdown
    * CAPEX profile, major cost element (e.g. external consultant cost, internal labor capitalisation, project cost transferred from other BUs.... etc).
    * Form of investment must be specified. (e.g. equity injection, shareholders loan, etc) 
### Associated Risk
    * The risks associated with the investment in the event of failure covering both financial and non-financial exposure; also contingency plans to mitigate the risks.
    * For new projects, minimum performance levels & exit plans should also be considered in case of performance shortfalls.

Financial Justification
As appropriate, the financial justification should consist of the profitability analysis (PA) of the investment as well as cashflow statement pertaining to the investment. In case the CAPEX / Investment applied is incremental, both the incremental and the total (including expenditure to date) Profitability Analysis should be prepared for comparison.
Where the application to incur capital investment involves cross-section/branches input, the input should be supported and confirmed by the branches involved. 
Joint efforts of the project initiating branches, engineering branches and other branches if applicable are required. For each project, the initiating branch will have to ensure the progress and development of the project applied can meet with their requirement while the supporting branch is accountable for all expenditure under his control.
The estimate of the costs and benefits of a project should show the differences that results from making the investment. The important information is the change in profitability and cashflows as a result of undertaking the project, i.e. the incremental principle.
For financial returns calculation, the following are suggested to be included where applicable:-
Internal rate of return 
The discount rate at which the putative value of the opportunity will be zero.
Net Present Value
 
The value calculated by projecting the future cashflow (CAPEX, Opex, Revenues) and discounting the value of future year's cash at the Discount Rate (as suggested below) to give its notional value today.
CAGR (Compounded Annual Growth Rate)
 
CAGR is the year over year growth rate of an investment over a specified period of time.
CAGR(for an investment of 5 years)
   
     
= (Value at Year 5 / Value at Year    1)^(1/4) - 1
Payback Period
 
The number of years of undiscounted cashflow required to cover acquisition costs and any other investment, i.e. when cumulative cashflow becomes positive.
Discount Rate (pre-tax)
 
Recommended rate at 20% (for both CAPEX and Investment projects).
Period of Assessment
 
Period of assessment should either be the project life or specific contract period. A period of 5 years (60 months) is highly recommended if no specific information is available.
Cost Items 
Cost of Sales / Services.
Operating costs include costs such as incremental network cost (hardware & software) or apportioned costs for use of network, sales, sales provision, sales support, computer bureau/billing, additional rentals, advertising, staff cost, contractor and service charges, financial charges, and depreciation.
Royalties.
Bad debts.
Space segment rentals.
Company Overhead
 Overhead should be applied to total operating costs including depreciation, but excluding any amounts relating to royalties, bad debts and space segment rental. Reference should be made to company overhead rates recommended by Group Finance - Regulatory Accounting Section.
Other incremental costs
 In identifying incremental costs it can often help to compare costs / benefits without the investment (i.e. do nothing) with those if the proposal proceeds.
Taxation - calculation of the profitability and cashflow for CAPEX project should be before corporate taxation and capital allowance effect.
Taxation impact should be considered in investment proposals that involves acquisition of equity in another company/set ups new offices....etc.
Notes & Assumptions
Assumptions made as to arrive at the proposed profitability and cashflow of the investment should be clearly listed out. Source of information should always be explicitly stated and dated for reference and cross-checking. Cross referenced to other functions and specific references should be mentioned.
 
forecast demand or activity net gains
forecast tariff rates on revenue and assumptions in arriving at such rate
breakdown of cost drivers for staff costs and other major expense items
inflation factor, usually the same one as used in AOP
For cost savings project, major assumptions to derive the cost savings
Cases must incorporate the effects of changes to the business environment that can be anticipated. Rarely is it appropriate to carry forward current prices and volumes unchanged, especially entering a period of significant regulatory, competitive and political change. E.g. Business case that use today’s IDD collection rates for the next five years are significantly over-optimistic. Conversely, costs are likely to be driven up by inflation. 
Sensitivity Analysis
 
Because of the inevitable uncertainty involved in forecasting, sensitivity analysis should be conducted on key success factors, i.e. the effect on the bottom line if key assumptions change, such as tariff changes, a certain percent variations in the demand forecast, early termination of contract etc. Such analysis usually takes the form of a table of alternatives to the base case assumptions, and consequent impact on IRR and NPV. A sensitivity analysis like this enables the OC to better understand the risk of the project.
If the project is being conducted and applied in phases, profitability and cashflow analysis for each phase and the accumulated total will be required, i.e. more than one scenario will be required. 
Business cases arriving at the OC with margins of 50% plus or IRRs of more than 100% will be regarded with suspicion and extra justification is required for such cases.
Appendices
 
Supporting documents which are considered necessary to facilitate management's assessment on the individual case e.g. activity forecast, traffic forecast, market study, cost or performance benchmarks, etc. should be attached to the business case as appendices. 
Means of Financing
 
Where appropriate, exploration of different means in acquiring capabilities and means of financing should be stated.
It is recommended to keep the submission paper as short as possible. Commercially sensitive information should be identified as a need to know basis.
