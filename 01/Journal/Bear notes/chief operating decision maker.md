# chief operating decision maker
The term 'chief operating decision maker' identifies a function, not necessarily a manager with a specific title. That **function is to allocate resources and to assess the performance of the entity's operating segments**. The chief operating decision maker of an entity is often the chief executive officer or chief operating officer but may be a group of executives or others.

An entity may produce reports for the chief operating decision maker in which its business activities are presented in a number of ways, for example by geographical region and by business type. This type of reporting may be referred to as matrix reporting; the same results are reported more than once, in this case by geographical region and also by business type.

**Only one set of components of an organisation can be presented as operating segments** in accordance with HKFRS 8. If the chief operating decision maker uses more than one set of segment information to make decisions, one of those sets should be identified as constituting an entity¶s operating segments by reference to factors such as:

- The nature of the business activities of each component; 
- The existence of managers responsible for them; and 
- Information presented to the board of directors.

If only one set of components of an entity has segment managers that are responsible for the components, that set constitutes the operating segments of the entity. An operating segment will generally have a segment manager who is directly accountable to and maintains regular contact with the chief operating decision maker to discuss operating, financial and strategic activities. The segment manager is a function and not necessarily an individual; in some cases a single manager is the segment manager for more than one segment.
