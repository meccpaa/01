# Strategic choice 
![](Strategic%20choice/E123FF96-801D-467B-AF42-6DFD08924A3C.png)

Strategic choices are made at both the corporate and business unit level. At the level of the business unit, these choices are about how to achieve **competitive advantage** and are based on an understanding of customers and markets. At the corporate level, strategy is primarily about scope: this is concerned with the **overall product/business portfolio**, the spread of markets and the relationship between business units and the corporate centre.

Strategic choices must also be made about the **direction and method of development**.
- - - -
- [[Strategic choice: Ansoff's matrix]] - Ansoff's product-market strategies involve determining which products should be sold in which markets, by market penetration, market development, product development and diversification.
[[Strategy self-test 2: Ansoff’s matrix]]

- [[Strategic choice: Porter's generic strategies]] - **Business unit** strategy involves a choice between being the lowest cost producer (cost leadership) making the product different from competitors' products in some way (differentiation) or specialising on a segment of the market (focus, by addressing that segment by a strategy of cost leadership or differentiation). Porter believes that a firm must choose one of these or be stuck-in-the-middle. 

- [[Strategic choice: Industry life cycle]] - The appropriate choice of strategy will be influenced by the relevant stage of the industry life cycle.

## Strategy evaluation and selection
Using models such as [[Strategic choice: Ansoff's matrix]] and [[Strategic choice: Porter's generic strategies]] a business can generate ideas for strategies to fill any gap identified. Such strategies then have to be **evaluated against the goals of the business** and a selection made regarding which ones to undertake.

### Stage 1: strategic options generation
Come up with new ideas on how to compete (competitive advantage), where to compete and method of growth
~Key tools, models, technique~
- [[Value chain analysis]]
- [[scenario]] building
- acquisition vs. Organic growth

### Stage 2: strategic options evaluation
Normally, each strategy has to be evaluated on the basis of:
- [[Acceptability - evaluation criteria]]
- [[Suitability - evaluation criteria]] 
- [[Feasibility - evaluation criteria]]
- Environmental fit

> Where the organisation does not have existing competencies or resources it may choose to implement a strategy through some form of joint development or strategic alliance rather than organic growth which might be ::slow and costly::.  

~Key tools, models, technique~
- [[stakeholders]] analysis
- risk analysis
- decision-making tools such as decision trees, matrices, ranking and scoring methods
- financial measures (e.g. ROCE, DCF)

### Stage 3: strategy selection
Involves choosing between the alternative strategies.

- Competitive strategies are the generic strategies for **competitive advantage** an organisation will pursue (which determine how you compete). [[Strategic choice: Porter's generic strategies]]
- **Product-market** strategies [[Strategic choice: Ansoff's matrix]] (which markets you should enter or leave) determine where you compete and the direction of growth.
- Institutional strategies (i.e. relationships with other organisations) determine the **method of growth.**
