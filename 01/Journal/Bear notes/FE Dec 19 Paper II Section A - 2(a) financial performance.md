# FE Dec 19 Paper II Section A - 2(a) financial performance
* **(a)**The Group experienced loss in the two recent years, and the Management called for a meeting to find out the reasons behind and discussed how to tackle the problems. Thomas looked at the financial statements and said the current ratio increased from 1.14 to 1.15 is at the acceptable level compared with the industry norm. Thomas concluded that the liquidity position of the Group improved; and using current ratio to assess the Group’s performance, he concluded that the liquidity position is reasonable but could not understand the reasons that the Group experiences loss. However, Paul disagreed to use one ratio to assess the Group’s performance. 
**Assume that you are Paul Lee, the Finance Director of the Group.**
	* **(i)  Explain to Thomas, in detail, the limitations of using one financial ratio to evaluate the Group’s performance by using other relevant ratios to support your analysis.**

	* **(ii)  Advise the limitations of using financial ratios in evaluating the Group’s performance and make recommendations on how to overcome the limitations.**
**(7 marks)**
- - - -
## Answer
(i) Thomas is correct that the current ratio increased and improved from Year 2017 to Year 2018; however, a single ratio may not be sufficient in evaluating the Group financial performance. 
### Liquidity 
For example, the quick ratio decreased from 0.9594 to 0.8713. It shows that the Group may have difficulty in paying off its current liabilities when they come due immediately. 
### Solvency 
For example, the debt to equity ratio increased from 3.1917 to 4.0183. A high debt to equity ratio is often viewed as high risk, as the Group has been aggressive in financing its growth with debt. 
### Efficiency 
For example, the inventory turnover increased from 185 days to 335 days. It shows that it takes more time to sell the inventories. The slow-moving and less liquid inventories will then occupy the storage space in the warehouse that increases the expense of the Group; and the inventories could become obsolete or worthless in the worst case 
For example, the asset turnover decreased from 0.35 to 0.32. It shows that the Group’s ability to use assets to generate revenue decreased 
### Profitability 
Regarding the profitability aspect of the Group, Gross Profit Margin, Return on Assets and Return on Equity etc are often used as indicators. However, analysts usually apply the Gross Profit Margin to compare the business models with competitors since different companies have different natures of expenses, such as selling and distribution expenses, administrative expenses and finance costs etc. 
With the exclusion of those expenses, the Management can focus at the costs of sales amount that can specifically figure out the ways to reduce the costs of sales and improve the profit of the Group. 
Therefore, the gross profit margin is used to assess a company’s business model by revealing the amount of profit left over from sales; and the gross profit margin of the Group has deteriorated from 0.3817 to 0.3677. 
A single ratio may not be useful in evaluating the Group’s financial performance; and a combination of ratios in different dimensions, such as, liquidity, solvency, efficiency, and profitability etc. is recommended. 

(ii) Also, financial ratios only reflect the past data and historical performance of a company; however, they do not cover non-financial data, such as, morale of employees, quality of furniture, quality of service (on time delivery), and number of complaints received etc. 
### Recommendations: 
The Management can consider using Balanced Scorecard (“BSC”) to overcome the limitations, as BSC provides the Management with a set of information that covers all relevant areas of performance (financial and non-financial) in an objective and unbiased way.
