# tax admin - self-test 2: omission
On 1 June 2016, Mr. Au joined Hello Co Ltd (Hello Co) with an accounting year-end date of 31 March, which is a private limited company incorporated in Hong Kong. Although Hello Co was a small manufacturing company, it held shares listed in Hong Kong for long-term investment purposes.

Mr. Au discovered that the IRD omitted to offset losses when assessing Hello Co's profits tax liabilities for the year of assessment 2013/14. The notice of assessment was issued in 2015. Apart from the loss set-off, Hello Co claimed initial and annual allowances in respect of moulds acquired for its own use in the tax year 2013/14.

In view of liquidity problems, Hello Co sold all its share investments (which it had held for five years) in the year of assessment 2014/15. The IRD assessed the gain and issued the 2014/15 notice of assessment last week.

Required

(a) Briefly discuss the actions that Mr. Au should take in respect of the notice of assessment for the year of assessment 2013/14.

(b) Briefly discuss the actions that Mr. Au should take in respect of the notice of assessment for the year of assessment 2014/15.

(c) In case Hello Co does not get a satisfactory result from the action in requirement (b), briefly discuss further action that can be taken.
- - - -
**Answer:**
(a) In respect of the notice of assessment 2013/14, an s.70A claim could be submitted for the following reasons:

(i) Treatment of moulds

Pursuant to s.16G of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO), a taxpayer who incurs specified capital expenditure on the provision of a prescribed fixed asset during the basis period for a year of assessment (year of assessment 1998/99 and onwards) is entitled to 100% deduction of the specified capital expenditure for that year of assessment.

As Hello Co used the moulds to manufacture goods, i.e. tools for manufacturing operations, it should be eligible to claim 100% deduction for the acquisition costs.

(ii) An assessment that has become final and conclusive can be re–opened under s.70A of the IRO if:

(1) An application is made within six years after the end of the year of assessment or within six months after the date on which the Notice of Assessment was served, whichever is the later; and

(2) It is established to the satisfaction of an assessor that the assessment is excessive due to an error or omission in a return or statement or that there is an arithmetical error or omission in the calculation of the assessable income or profits or tax charged.

However, no correction will be made under s.70A of the IRO if the assessment was issued in accordance with the practice generally prevailing at the time the return or statement was made.

Omission of the loss set–off by the IRD should be regarded as an arithmetical omission in the ascertainment of assessable profits. At the same time, Hello Co made an error of fact in the 2013/14 profits tax return. It claimed initial and annual allowances (instead of 100% deduction) in relation to the moulds acquired in the tax year 2013/14. Under these circumstances, Hello Co should apply for re–opening the assessment under s.70A for the year of assessment 2013/14.

- - - -
(b) If Hello Co disagrees with an assessment raised by an assessor, it can lodge an objection with the Commissioner of Inland Revenue (CIR).

The notice of objection shall:

(i) Be in writing;

(ii) State precisely the grounds for the objection; and

(iii) Be received by the CIR within one month after the issue date of the 2014/15 Notice of Assessment.

Hello Co is required to prove that the assessment is incorrect or excessive.
- - - -

(c) If the CIR rejects the argument submitted by Hello Co regarding the gain on disposal of listed shares, Hello Co can give notice of appeal to the Board of Review (BoR). The requirements for a valid appeal are outlined as follows:

(i) The notice must be in writing to the Clerk of the BoR.

(ii) The notice must be given within one month after CIR's determination of the objection.

(iii) The notice must be accompanied by a statement of the grounds of appeal and a copy of CIR's written determination together with copies of the reasons therefor and of the statement of facts.

(iv) When Hello Co gives the notice of appeal to the BoR, it must, at the same time, send copies of it, and of the statement of the grounds of appeal, to the CIR.
