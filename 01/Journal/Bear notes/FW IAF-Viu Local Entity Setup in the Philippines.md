NewCo

It is proposed to set up NewCo as a wholly owned subsidiary of PCCW OTT SG to support Viu OTT PH. NewCo can be 100% foreign owned, and once established and registered, NewCo is free to carry out any business activities in Philippines as indicated in its Articles of Incorporation (other than activities subject to foreign nationality restrictions – see below).

NewCo, will also be able to enter into employment contracts, leases and other important commercial contracts to support the growth of the Viu OTT PH.  This would particularly be helpful to the local team on the ground, as for example, BU once informed GLO that it encountered difficultly in convincing a prospective landlord to enter into a lease arrangement with PCCW OTT SG because the team has no proof of local business registration/license that it can present to the prospective landlord when the landlord requested such documents.

In addition, the distributorship contracts with local telecom companies, depending on how such contracts are drafted, may risk PCCW OTT SG to be seen by the relevant authorities as operating a business locally in PH and risk being seen as carrying out business in Philippines without a business registration.   Therefore, to mitigate any such risks, such distributorship contracts should be novated from PCCW OTT SG to NewCo once established, and BU should also evaluate whether other commercial contracts with local entities should also be signed by NewCo going forward to minimize similar legal concerns.  According to the tax team, the assignment of the relevant contracts may give rise to a transfer of business situation (although no capital gains tax are payable in Singapore, a sale and purchase agreement would be needed to document the process).  As discussed with BU and, given that the only major distributorship contract expires this year by the end of September, (as NewCo likely will not be formally established until July/August the earliest) its preference is to save the hassle and to just let this contract and the other similar contracts run their respective courses and then sign new contracts using NewCo when it comes time for renewal (which, as confirmed by tax team, would then avoid triggering any transfer of business situation). Another way to avoid a transfer of business scenario, if necessary, would be to early terminate the relevant contracts and sign new ones with the NewCo (also confirmed by tax team).  BU is advised to keep abreast of the regulatory situation to continually assess whether there is a sudden urgent need to take the said early termination route or whether it is okay to let the existing contracts run its course and then sign with the NewCo.

Adrian
