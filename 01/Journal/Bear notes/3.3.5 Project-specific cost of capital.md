# 3.3.5 Project-specific cost of capital
The application of MM's theories to calculating a cost of capital for a specific investment project is described in Chapter 13. It may be helpful to link MM theory on cost of capital and gearing with MM's theories on cost of capital and investment appraisal.

(a) Financial risk is higher for a geared company than for an all-equity company, and the cost of equity is higher to allow for this greater risk. The ß value of equity in a geared company is higher than the ß value of the equity in a similar ungeared company.

(b) When the business risk in one capital investment project is higher than the business risk in another investment, the investor should expect a higher return from the higher-risk investment. The systematic risk and the beta factor for the higher-risk investment are higher than for the lower-risk investment.

When a company is considering whether to invest in a project that will change its capital structure and/or which will involve a different level of business risk, it should not use its existing WACC as the cost of capital for DCF analysis. Instead it should use a project-specific cost of capital.

There are **two methods** which can be used to calculate a project specific cost of capital. Both methods begin by calculating an ungeared beta for the company's equity.

### Method 1 of calculating project-specific cost of equity
There are three steps in this approach.

(a) Step 1: Obtain information about the industry in which the company is considering whether to invest. Use this information about the industry to calculate an ungeared beta factor for the industry.
![](Journal/Bear%20notes/Doc/3.3.5%20Project-specific%20cost%20of%20capital/277C8696-2720-487B-B614-736531280AD6.png)

(b) Step 2: Having calculated the ungeared beta for the industry, use the CAPM to calculate the cost of equity in an ungeared company in the industry.

(c) Step 3: Finally, calculate a cost of capital that the company should use for a DCF evaluation of the investment. This can be calculated assuming that the company will not change its capital structure. MM's formula for the adjusted cost of capital should be used.
