# Capital structure Example: Method 1 project specific cost of capital
DEF Company is a geared company with a debt-equity ratio of 2:3 by market values. The company’s debt is risk-free, and yields 4% before tax. The beta value of the company's equity is currently 1.10. The average returns on stock market equity are 12%.

DEF is now proposing to invest in a project which would involve diversification into a new industry. The following information is available about this industry.

(a) Average beta coefficient of equity capital = 0.75

(b) Average debt-equity ratio in the industry (by market values) = 1:4 The rate of tax is 20%. What would be a suitable cost of capital to apply to the project, assuming that DEF would not change its capital structure when financing the new investment?
- - - -
Step 1: Use information about the industry to calculate an ungeared beta for the industry.
![](Capital%20structure%20Example%20Method%201%20project%20specific%20cost%20of%20capital/99FB6F98-30EF-4E73-B5B8-06C0BF0A31AE.png)

Step 2: Next, use this ungeared beta value and the CAPM to calculate the cost of equity in an ungeared company in the industry:
Keu = 4% + 0.625 (12 – 4)% = 9%.

Step 3: Finally, use the ungeared cost of equity to calculate an adjusted cost of equity, assuming that DEF will not change its capital structure if it invests in the industry.
![](Capital%20structure%20Example%20Method%201%20project%20specific%20cost%20of%20capital/82F1ECC4-B249-4B03-8747-275C1BE32076.png)
The cost of capital for evaluating the investment should be 8.28%. The company may round this up to 8.3% or possibly 9%.
