# Sustainability reporting: Global Reporting Initiative
There has been some progress towards standardisation of CSR reports, so that reports of different companies can be compared more easily – especially reports of companies in the same industry.

The Global Reporting Initiative (GRI), founded in 1997, is the world-wide standard-setter in sustainability reporting. Its mission is 'to make sustainability reporting standard practice for all organisations'. It defines sustainability reporting as 'the practice of measuring, disclosing, and being accountable to internal and external stakeholders for organisational performance towards the goal of sustainable development'. The GRI's reporting guidelines are a global voluntary code which provide a standard framework for organisations to report on sustainability. The GRI format for sustainability reporting is a 'triple bottom line' approach to reporting on economic, environmental and social performance:

- Economic – concerns the organisation's impacts on the economic conditions of its stakeholders and on economic systems at local, national, and global levels.
- Environmental – concerns an organisation's impacts on living and non-living natural systems, including ecosystems, land, air, and water.
- Social – concerns the impacts an organisation has on the social systems within which it operates.

The GRI's latest G4 guidelines, published in 2013, emphasise the need for organisations to focus their reports on the information that is material to their business and their key stakeholders i.e. the aspects which reflect the organisation's significant economic, environmental and social impacts; or that will influence the assessments and decisions of stakeholders.

A sustainability report based on the GR4 Guidelines has several sections which include General Standard disclosures on:
- Strategy 
- Profile of the organisation 
- Identified material aspects and boundaries for the report 
- Engagement with stakeholders 
- Governance 
- Ethics and integrity.

### Case study 1: sustainability policies and sustainability reporting

A number of Hong Kong companies have produced annual CSR reports/sustainability reports for several years. These include, for example, NWS Holdings Ltd, Hong Kong and Shanghai Hotels Ltd, Fuji Xerox (Hong Kong), CLP Group and HK Electric.

The 2014 sustainability report of energy company CLP Group was based on the GR4 guidelines of the Global Reporting Initiative. CLP's vision is to be 'the leading responsible energy provider in the Asia-Pacific region'. Its report is mainly narrative in form, but also contains quantified performance statistics on environmental, employee, governance, social and financial performance that are independently verified by an external auditing agency.

CLP's 2014 sustainability report includes performance statistics (key performance indicators or KPIs) for each of the years 2010 – 2014 on the following aspects of environmental and safety performance.
- Use of resources and emissions
	- Consumption of coal, gas and oil 
	- Emissions of gases such as CO2 and SO2 
	- Water withdrawal 
	- Water discharged 
	- Waste produced (hazardous and non-hazardous) 
	- Waste recycled (hazardous and non-hazardous)

- Electricity generation
	- Total renewable energy generation capacity 
	- Non-carbon emitting generation capacity
	
- Safety
	- Number of fatalities 
	- Lost time: number of injuries 
	- Total recordable injury rate 
	- Days lost

CLP's disclosure efforts have been widely recognised both in Hong Kong and internationally. In 2014 for example, the company won the HKICPA Sustainability and Social Responsibility Reporting Award for the fourth year in a row, and the Hong Kong Management Association's Australasian-Hong Kong Sustainability Reporting Award for the second consecutive year. It also received the 2014 ARA (Australasian Reporting Awards) Special Award for Sustainability Reporting.

### Case study 2: sustainability policies and sustainability reporting 
HK Electric is another company that publishes a sustainability report. Its narrative reporting includes the comments: ‘conducting business in a responsible and sustainable way is no longer an option but a must. At HK Electric, our CSR and environmental policies and initiatives reflect our unwavering commitment to minimising the environmental impact of our operations, while at the same time contributing to our community… The wellbeing of staff, customers, contractors and other stakeholders remains an ongoing focus at HK Electric…'

HK Electric's 2014 sustainability report, like the report of CLP, is largely narrative but with some quantitative performance measures on items such as: consumption of coal, gas and oil; air emissions; ash produced and ash sold for industrial use; waste oils recycled and waste oil for disposal; water consumption and discharge; fatalities; time lost to injuries, and so on.
