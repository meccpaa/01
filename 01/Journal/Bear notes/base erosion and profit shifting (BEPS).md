# base erosion and profit shifting (BEPS)
On October 26 2016, the Hong Kong Government launched a consultation exercise to gauge views on implementation of measures by the OECD to counter BEPS of enterprises.

The OECD put forward 15 action plans to tackle BEPS, which seek to improve the coherence of international tax rules, realign taxation with economic substance and value creation, and promote a transparent tax environment. The OECD's top priority is to **monitor the implementation of the four minimum standards**, i.e.
-  ~countering harmful tax practices~, 
- preventing treaty abuse, 
- imposing country-by-country (CbC) reporting requirements and 
- improving the cross-border dispute resolution regime.

Hong Kong will focus on the four minimum standards of BEPS (Actions 5, 6, 13 and 14) as well as measures of direct relevance to the implementation.

The salient points of the four minimum standards include:
Action 5 – Review of harmful tax practices and spontaneous exchange of information on certain tax rulings.

Action 6 – Some of the Hong Kong's treaty partners have requested Hong Kong include the ~simplified Limitation of Benefits rule and Principal Purposes Test~ in the relevant Hong Kong tax treaties to prevent tax treaty abuse. In other words, "**substance**" must be in place to enjoy tax treaty benefits. Going forward, Hong Kong is likely to incorporate the simplified Limitation of Benefits rule and Principal Purposes Test in negotiating and concluding tax treaties.

Action 13 – Hong Kong needs to put in place ~comprehensive transfer pricing legislation and documentation~ requirements. Transfer pricing documentation requirements include CbC reporting and the exchange of CbC reports.

Action 14 – Hong Kong needs to improve ~cross-border tax dispute resolution~ and implement a statutory mechanism for dealing with mutual agreement procedure.

On top of the above, Hong Kong needs to introduce a multilateral instrument (Action 15) for ease of amending the Hong Kong tax treaties to implement BEPS measures.
