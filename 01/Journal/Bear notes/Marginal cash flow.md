# Marginal cash flow
Marginal cash flow shows the net of the variable cash inflows generated by operations after financing the variable working capital used by these operations. Marginal cash flow is the difference between the margin of a product and the marginal working capital required to support the sale of the product which includes trade receivables and inventory, less trade payables required for the next unit of product or service. It helps to indicate what is likely to happen to cash flow in the future if these fundamental relationships are maintained.

The Marginal Cash Flow (MCF) calculation is:
MCF = contribution margin – change in working capital

(where the change in working capital is an increase in working capital, if not any decrease is added rather than subtracted).
