# Standardised and automated working papers
The use of standardised working papers, for example, checklists and specimen letters, may improve the efficiency of audit work but they can be dangerous because they may lead to auditors mechanically following an approach without using audit judgment.

Automated working paper packages have been developed which can make the documenting of audit work much easier. Such programs aid preparation of working papers, lead schedules, the trial balance and the financial statements themselves. These are automatically cross-referenced, adjusted and balanced by the computer.

The advantages of automated working papers are as follows:

(a) The risk of misstatements is reduced

(b) The working papers will be neater and easier to review

(c) The time saved will be substantial as adjustments can be made easily to all working papers, including those summarising the key analytical information

(d) Standard forms do not have to be carried to audit locations

(e) Audit working papers can be transmitted for review via email or fax facilities
