Inventory is often a major area of importance for the auditor and, historically, has been the component of the statement of financial position that creates more problems than any other. There are a number of reasons for this including the reasons stated below:
	- Inventory is usually a **significant balance** in the calculation of profit and a material component of the statement of financial position
	- The determination of ::year-end quantities:: can be problematic
	- There are ::different approaches:: to its **valuation** and estimation and subjective assessment is usually required
	- There is an increased risk of ::manipulation:: and fraud by management and relevant parties

## 3.6 Valuation of inventory
The valuation and disclosure rules for inventory are laid down in [[HKAS 2 Inventories]]. Inventory should be valued at the **lower of cost and net realisable value**.

The auditor needs to consider the valuation of inventories by reviewing whether:
	- The entity has followed HKAS 2 when accounting the value for inventories (i.e. lower of cost or net realisable value)
	- The entity has allocated the **overheads** appropriately 
	- The entity has applied the method of accounting for inventories **consistently**
