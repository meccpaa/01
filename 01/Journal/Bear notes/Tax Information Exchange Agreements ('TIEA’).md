# Tax Information Exchange Agreements ('TIEA’)
The Inland Revenue (Amendment) Bill 2013 was introduced on 12 April 2013. Under the proposed amendments, Hong Kong can enter into a TIEA with other jurisdictions simply for the purpose of exchange of tax information in relation to any tax imposed by the laws of Hong Kong or that jurisdiction. In addition, persons who do not possess but have control of the information will also be obligated to supply the information upon request. The Bill was subsequently passed on 10 July 2013. The Bill enables Hong Kong to enter into TIEAs with other jurisdictions where necessary and to enhance the EoI arrangements in terms of the coverage of tax types and the limitation on disclosure of information under its DTAs.

On 25 March 2014, Hong Kong signed a TIEA with the United States. This is the first TIEA signed by Hong Kong.

The HK-US TIEA provides the necessary basis for Hong Kong to provide for EoI upon requests made in relation to the information reported by financial institutions in Hong Kong to the U.S. tax authorities under the US Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act ('FATCA').

FATCA requires US persons, including those who live outside the US, to report to the US tax authorities their financial accounts held in other jurisdictions, and requires foreign financial institutions including those in Hong Kong to report the financial information in respect of their US clients. The HK-US TIEA came into force from 24 June 2014.

On 10 December 2014, the Hong Kong Government exchanged notes with the Japan Government to expand the coverage of tax types under the Exchange of Information arrangement of the avoidance of double taxation agreement signed between Hong Kong and Japan. The exchange of notes between Hong Kong and Japan entered into force from 6 July 2015.

As of 1 June 2017, Hong Kong has concluded seven TIEAs with the United States, Denmark, the Faroes, Greenland, Iceland, Norway and Sweden.
