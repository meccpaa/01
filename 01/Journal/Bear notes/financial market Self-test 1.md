# financial market: Self-test 1
On November 5 1987, the Hang Seng index fell by over 40%. Does the existence of such periodic stock market crashes support or contradict the concept of market efficiency?

- - - -
## Answer 1

There is no precise definition of a market crash, but is a phenomenon of international stock markets that they can suddenly fall dramatically over a short period of time. Stock market crashes are dramatic falls in indices such as those which occurred in 1929, 1987 or 2008. To decide whether such crashes are consistent with ideas of market efficiency we have to consider the reasons for them.

If a crash occurs due to the public availability of new information such as very poor economic data, or due to a dramatic event such as a sovereign debt default, then it could be argued that the crash is the result of rational investors factoring in the lower expected returns into the valuation of investments and could be consistent with semi-strong efficiency. However, even if a crash can be attributed to a specific event, such as the reaction of global markets to the collapse of US bank, Lehman Brothers, in 2008 this would argue against the existence of strong form efficiency, since for this to hold true share prices would already reflect the information that this event represented.

If crashes occur without the scale of the market fall being clearly attributable to new information or events this questions whether they are the result of rational investor behaviour, which is one of the assumptions implicit in market efficiency. In a crash investor behaviour may be driven by emotional or behavioural factors such as investor panic. It can be argued that investors who sell out of fear, solely because shares are falling are not acting rationally and in doing so amplify market falls. Share price falls driven by such factors are not consistent with the efficient market hypothesis.
