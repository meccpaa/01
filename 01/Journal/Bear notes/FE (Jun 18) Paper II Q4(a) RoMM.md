# FE (Jun 18) Paper II: Q4(a) RoMM
(a) Identify, with explanations, five financial statement line items of WT Tours you consider may have a risk of material misstatement and state the respective additional information you would obtain from the management for further risk assessment.

Note: You are not required to state the audit procedures that you plan to perform.

(10 marks)
- - - -
- Revenue
- Trade receivables 
- - - -
## Answer
### (i) Trade receivables
Trade receivables approximate to 22% of current assets, which looks material to the company’s financial information. The full amount will be paid by the customers as deposits when they join the tour so it is unlikely there will be any trade receivables.

Additional information: listing / breakdown of trade receivables, aging analysis, provision policy, etc.

### (ii) Customer deposits / Deferred revenue / Revenue
Deposits received in advance from the customers should be recognised as a liability – deferred revenue. While the amount recognised as customer deposits may represent cash received from customers, there is no such balance in the liability to match with these deposits received. It is likely it has already been recognised as revenue leading to a risk of overstatement of revenue.

Additional information: breakdown of customer deposits to match with the revenue recognised, etc.

### (iii) Other assets
Except for fixed assets, trade receivables and customers deposits, the company has no other assets. Also, although the industrial building in Shatin is leased out to an independent third party for rental income, no investment property is recognised, it is likely that it is recognised as fixed assets.

Additional information: details of fixed assets recognised, a list of other prepayment and deposits (such as utilities deposits), rental agreement (if any, to identify if any deposits have been paid), etc.

### (iv) Bank loans
There is a risk that bank loans are not recognised completely and / or properly recognised as non-current / current liabilities.

Additional information: bank loan agreements, banking facilities letters, broad minutes (to understand if there are any other bank loans), etc.

### (v) Accounts payables / Accruals / Expenses
The company has only HK$11 million trade payables and there are no other payables, accruals, provisions, etc.

Additional information: breakdown of trade payables, list of subsequent payments (to identify if there are any unrecorded liabilities), etc.

### (vi) Provision for litigation
No provision for litigation was recognised. Also, the potential exposures of some litigations were considered as material but nothing was disclosed in the notes to the financial statements.

Also, there may be a risk of going concern, depending on the quantitative and qualitative impact arising from the litigations.

Additional information: details and status of litigations, other valid point

### (vii) Tax liabilities / Tax expenses
There is no provision for tax.

Additional information: detail of different types of tax that the company is subject to (e.g. Hong Kong tax, overseas tax), management’s tax assessment, etc.

### (viii) Intercompany balances
There are several intercompany transactions but there are no intercompany balances recognised on the statement of financial position.

Additional information: details of intercompany transactions, movement of intercompany balances, etc.
