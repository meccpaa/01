# Profits tax - example 84 industrial building allowance
Example 84 (Adapted from DIPN 2, Example 3)

C constructed a building at a cost of $800,000 in September 2012 and used it as a workshop in his existing manufacturing trade. He makes up accounts to 31 December.

In January 2015, he sold the building for $700,000 to D, who used it for a time as a showroom in his existing manufacturing business. In May 2017, D converted it to a workshop again. D makes up accounts to 31 March. (Site values are excluded throughout.)

![](Profits%20tax%20-%20example%2084%20industrial%20building%20allowance/image.png)
![](Profits%20tax%20-%20example%2084%20industrial%20building%20allowance/image%202.png)
Had D used the building for a qualifying purpose throughout, his first year of allowance would have been 2014/15, and the allowance would be $700,000 / 24 = $29,167.

D, however, does not qualify for industrial building allowances until 2017/18. So no one gets an allowance for 2015/16 and 2016/17. Notional allowances must therefore be written off for 2015/16 and 2016/17 at an amount of 4% of the capital expenditure. Notional allowance need not be written off for 2014/15 because C received an annual allowance for that year.

![](Profits%20tax%20-%20example%2084%20industrial%20building%20allowance/image%203.png)

D would get commercial building allowance of $32,000 ($800,000  4%) (calculated using the same formula above) for each of the years of assessment 2014/15, 2015/16 and 2016/17. This has no effect on the industrial building allowance.
