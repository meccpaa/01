# The Hong Kong debt and equity markets
In the international bond markets, interest rates have been low, especially in the USA, Japan and Europe. International investors have increased their investments in other bond markets, in search of higher yields.

The bond market in Hong Kong has continued to develop. A large proportion of bonds issued by Hong Kong corporates have been denominated in US dollars. The peg between the Hong Kong dollar and US dollar reduces the foreign exchange risk for the bond issuers. An increasing proportion of bond issues have been made to refinance maturing debt or to repay outstanding debt.

However, in December 2016 the US Federal reserve increased interest rates for the first time since the global financial crisis. The Federal reserve again increased rates in March 2017 and have provided guidance to expect an upward trajectory for interest rates for 2017 and 2018. The existence of the US dollar peg compelled the HKMA to match these increases in the US rates and other countries may follow suit and increase interest rates as the global economy recovers. These increases in interest rates are considered by many to spell the end of the long term bull market for bonds.

A publicised development in the Hong Kong equity markets was the proposed flotation in 2013 of China's largest IT company, Alibaba.

**Alibaba** entered into discussions with Hong Kong regulators (the Hong Kong Securities and Futures Commission) about the possibility of an IPO on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. Negotiations failed because Alibaba demanded a **corporate governance structure** in which **senior management** of the company (executives on the board) would ~control appointments to the board~, through a **two-class equity structure** that gave effective control to a small management group. The SFC ruled that this was **unacceptable**, and that all shareholders should have equal rights, with one vote per share, over board appointments.

Listing by Alibaba on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange would have created benefits from greater prestige for the exchange, fee income for financial institutions and higher trading volumes in the secondary equities market. The SFC took the view that it was more important to protect smaller shareholders and standards of corporate governance in Hong Kong, as this would benefit Hong Kong in the longer term as a global financial centre. In September 2014 Alibaba floated in the US, reaching a market capitalisation of **over $230 billion** to become the world's ~largest ever stock market flotation~. The previous record holder was Agricultural Bank of China, which raised $22bn in its 2010 listing in Hong Kong.
