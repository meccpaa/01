# Capital structure Example: Impact of gearing on Market value, cost of equity and WACC
Company ABC currently has no debt capital in its capital structure. The company's 100 million shares have a current value of $800 million. The company proposes to raise an additional $200 million for a major new investment that would not affect the business risk profile of the company's operations (and so would not affect the beta value of equity or the cost of equity). The rate of tax relief on debt interest is 16.5%.

The risk-free rate of return is 4% and the average market return is 9%. The beta factor for the shares in ABC is 0.80. Assume that the cost of debt capital for ABC would be the same as the riskfree rate of return, if the company issues bonds.

Required

According to MM theory, what would happen to the market value of the company, the cost of equity and the WACC if the company chooses to raise the new capital by issuing bonds? (Ignore transaction costs with issuing shares or debt.)
- - - -
## Solution
**Total market value**
If the company issues new shares, the total value of the all-equity financed company after the share issue is assumed to be $800 million + $200 million = $1,000 million.

If instead the company issues $200 million of bonds, the total market value of the company will be:
![](Capital%20structure%20Example%20Impact%20of%20gearing%20on%20Market%20value,%20cost%20of%20equity%20and%20WACC/DFAD4066-2BD9-48AB-8422-2FBA54361B0D.png)
Of this total market value, $200 million would be debt capital. Therefore the total value of equity would be $833 million. Hence existing shareholders, who currently have shares worth $800 million would benefit by $33 million from the decision to issue debt rather than new equity. This is because the tax shield effectively provides more earnings that can be distributed to shareholders.

**Cost of equity**
Using CAPM, the current cost of equity in ABC (ungeared) is 4% + 0.80 (9 – 4)% = 8%.

It is assumed that if the company raises capital by issuing new shares, this cost of equity will remain the same, since the major new investment would not affect the business risk profile of the company's operations.

If the company issues debt capital and not equity, the cost of equity will change to allow for the financial risk.

The cost of equity would increase from 8% to 8.8019%.
