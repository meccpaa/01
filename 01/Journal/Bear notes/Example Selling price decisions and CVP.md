# Example: Selling price decisions and CVP
A company operates with a contribution/sales ratio of 60%. Annual fixed costs are $900,000 and the company makes an annual profit of $300,000. It has been estimated that:

(a) If sales prices are increased by 20%, sales volume would fall by 10%, and

(b) If sales prices are reduced by 10%, sales volume would increase by 20%. Required Would a change in sales prices result in higher profit?

## Solution
Currently total annual contribution = $900,000 + $300,000 = $1,200,000.

C/S ratio = 0.60.

Therefore annual sales are currently $1,200,000/0.60 = $2,000,000.

(a) If the sales price is increased by 20%, the C/S ratio would change as follows:
![](Example%20Selling%20price%20decisions%20and%20CVP/11625D1E-DC46-4FB7-BCC8-C01D4172DEF8.png)
The new C/S ratio will be 80/120 or 2/3.

Sales volume will fall by 10% so revenue will fall from $2,000,000 to $1,800,000. Sales prices will rise by 20% so revenue will rise from $1,800,000 to $2,160,000.

Total contribution will be $2,160,000  2/3 = $1,440,000.This means that total contribution rises; therefore a price increase by 20% would increase total contribution and profit would increase from $300,000 to $540,000.

(b) If the sales price is reduced by 10%, the C/S ratio would change as follows:
![](Example%20Selling%20price%20decisions%20and%20CVP/5E4880CC-E079-4739-B3B4-B69D563E87F8.png)

The new C/S ratio will be 50/90 or 5/9.

Sales volume will increase revenue by 20% from $2,000,000 to $2,400,000. Sales prices will fall by 10% so revenue will fall from $2,400,000 to $2,160,000.

Total contribution will be $2,160,000 x 5/9 = $1,200,000.

This means a price reduction of 10% would not increase total contribution and profit. In fact, total profit and contribution will remain the same.
