# FE (Jun 18) Paper I: Section A Q3(b) listing transaction costs accounting
(b) The accounting treatments for the transaction costs listed below if Grand Music Group is seeking an initial public offerings (“IPO”) which involves a concurrent listing of existing shares and issuance of new shares.

- Underwriting fees
- Sponsor’s fees
- Tax and legal entity restructuring costs in anticipation of the IPO
- Legal and tax advice fees (other than those relating to restructuring in the IPO above)
- Reporting accountants’ fees relating to the prospectus
- Prospectus preparation, design and printing costs
- Road show costs, press conferences and advertising or promotional activity costs
(5 marks)
- - - -
## answer
In accordance with HKAS 32 Financial Instruments, the transaction costs of an equity transaction are accounted for as **a deduction from equity** to the extent they are **incremental costs** ~directly attributable~ to the equity transaction that otherwise would have been avoided. The costs of an equity transaction that is abandoned are recognised as an expense.
[[HKAS 32: Presentation of financial instruments]]

Transaction costs that **relate jointly to more than one transaction** (for example, costs of a ~concurrent offering~ of some shares and a stock exchange listing of other shares) are allocated to those transactions using a basis of allocation that is [[rational]] and consistent with similar transactions.

Factors for considering whether the selected basis of allocation is “rational” include but are not limited to:
- An allocation between the listing of existing shares and issue of new shares should not result in costs attributed to either of the two components being greater than the costs that would be incurred if either were a standalone transaction.
- Whether the extent of work involved would have been different if only one part of the transaction was undertaken.

A c ~ommonly adopted practice~ of allocating the jointly incurred costs is based on the **proportion of number of new shares issued** ~relative to~ the **total number of shares in issue and listed after IPO** (i.e. new and existing shares, which have been listed through the IPO).

**Underwriting fees** are one example of transaction costs which are accounted for as a ~deduction~ from equity since the underwriting fees are incremental costs directly attributable to the issue of **new shares** to raise funds by the listing entity during the IPO.

Meanwhile, tax and legal entity restructuring costs in ~anticipation of the IPO~ and ~road show costs, press conferences and advertising or promotional activity costs~ are examples of transaction costs that are ::solely related to the listing of existing shares:: or other activities undertaken ~at the same time as the share issue~ (which do not relate to the issue of new shares for funding) are accounted for as **expenses** and recognised in profit or loss as incurred.

For costs that relate **jointly to both** the issuance of ~new~ shares and the listing of the ~existing~ shares which should be ~**allocated**~ to the transaction that is rational and consistent with similar transactions.

Examples of these costs include **sponsor’s fees, legal and tax advice fees (other than those relating to restructuring in IPO above)**, reporting accountants’ fees relating to prospectus and prospectus preparation, design and printing costs.
