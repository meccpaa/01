# FE (Jun 18) Paper I: case
The Grand Music International (B.V.I.) Company Limited and its subsidiaries (“Grand Music Group”, or the “Group”) were founded by Mr Julian Wong, a singer, and a group of musicians in Hong Kong in 2000. The Board of Directors comprises Mr Julian Wong and two other major shareholders [[Corporate governance]]. It aims at providing a platform for all styles of music as well as a quality learning environment for different musicians. The Grand Music Group has its financial year-end on 31 March [[Basis period]].

The group structure of Grand Music Group as at 31 March 2017 was as follows:
![](FE%20(Jun%2018)%20Paper%20I%20case/6AE8A0E5-81C9-4C66-991C-7B77714E6F76.png)

~**Music Land (H.K.) Company Limited**~
Music Land (H.K.) Company Limited (“Music Land”) is a company incorporated in Hong Kong and it operates the brand of Music Land in either ~rented units in shopping malls~ [[HKFRS 16 Leases]] in Hong Kong / Shenzhen or self-owned units in commercial buildings in Hong Kong. Music Land provides flexible rental services ranging from hourly rental to monthly rental for:

(i) piano room, band room, drum room, etc; and
(ii) musical instruments for courses, practising, or events such as a wedding party or a musical performance.

The **full deposits** amount has to be ~paid in advance~ once the booking is confirmed.

The **musical instruments** used by Music Land are mainly purchased from Best Performance (HK) Company Limited (“Best Performance”), which are of ~high quality and some of them are quite expensive~. During the year, Music Land **donated** certain new and used musical instruments with a ~carrying amount of approximately HK$5 million~ to schools, childcare centres and service organisations. No such donation has been made previously. [[s.26C: Approved charitable donations]]

Music Land owns a unit in an **industrial building** [[Depreciation allowances]] in Kwai Chung, which is used as the offices for the Group companies as well as the warehouse for the consignment [[Consignment arrangements]] and normal sales of the musical instruments by Best Performance at Music Land. Music Land displays Best Performance’s musical instruments in its branches and ~20% of the invoiced~ amount will be charged by Music Land for the **consignment sales**. 

Music Land has various **in-house developed systems** for room-booking, rental of musical instruments, consignment and normal sales as well as book-keeping for financial reporting. However, there is ::no interface:: between the systems. Since ~manual reconciliations~ between different systems have to be performed, certain operational and finance personnel have access to different systems to extract relevant information to perform the reconciliations and to adjust the data if any error is identified in the reconciliations.

~**Fantastic Music (H.K.) Company Limited**~

Fantastic Music (H.K.) Company Limited (“Fantastic”) is a subsidiary of Music Land. Students can join a **one-hour class** without any commitment and pay each time at a fee of **HK$800**. Fantastic has over 150 instructors and they are either full-time or part-time employees of Fantastic. An **additional 20% of the fee** will be charged if ~a specific instructor~ is selected. Fantastic shares the fee with instructors on a negotiated basis.

In addition, Fantastic offers **different kinds of courses**. It offers ~single 10-lesson~ and ~20-lesson~ one-hour private class courses for playing different musical instruments in Music Land. The course fees of the 10-lesson course and 20-lesson course are HK$6,000 and HK$12,000 respectively, which include the tuition fee of the instructors, rental services of the music room and / or the musical instruments (if necessary) to be used in the class. The course fee should be **fully paid in advance** before the commencement of the first lesson and it is **non-refundable** under any circumstance unless Fantastic ::fails to perform::. The 10-lesson course must be completed ~within three months~, with a maximum of one lesson per week. The 20-lesson course must be completed ~within six months~, with a maximum of two lessons per week. As a kind of incentive, students can enjoy **one extra lesson free-of-charge** if they join the 20-lesson course.

In addition, based on the customary business practices of the Group, **10% and 15% discount coupons** are given for students who join the 10-lesson and 20-lesson courses, respectively, to purchase musical instruments from the Group and the coupon is effective one month from the date of joining the course. Normally the Group ~would only give a 5% discount~ to students who ::do not join a course::. Based on Fantastic’s experience, **80%** of the students will ~redeem~ the coupons before expiry.

Other than instructors, Fantastic does not have any other employees. The daily operation and the accounting records are ~managed by Music Land~ and a ~monthly management fee~ is charged by Music Land on a **cost-plus basis**.

~**Business Plan of Grand Music Group**~
Grand Music Group has expanded quickly in the last ten years. Music Land has more than 30 branches in Hong Kong / Shenzhen and it is also expanding to other cities such as Guangzhou, Shanghai and Beijing. Fantastic has more than 800 students. Starting from 2000, the performance bonus has been calculated with reference to key performance indicators such as the rent-out rate of Music Land and the number of students accepted by Fantastic.

In order to further expand the Group, Mr Julian Wong is planning for the **listing of the Group** on the Main Board or the Growth Enterprise Market (“GEM”) of the Stock Exchange in Hong Kong. The Group is also considering to ~acquire Best Performance~, a major supplier of Grand Music Group.

~**Best Performance (HK) Company Limited**~
Best Performance is a company incorporated in Hong Kong with several overseas branches. The principal activities of Best Performance are the ~selling of musical instruments and co-ordination of orchestras~ for musical performances.

The group structure of Best Performance as at 31 March 2017 was as follows:
![](FE%20(Jun%2018)%20Paper%20I%20case/88E3515A-4467-4678-ADC5-32E0BA66346A.png)
The overseas branches of Best Performance purchase musical instruments from different countries, then deliver the same to a Hong Kong warehouse operated by a ~third party~. Some Hong Kong employees would be sent to work for the overseas branches for **three to six months every year** to assist with the purchase process. The staff based in Hong Kong would then arrange to ~sell to customers~ in Hong Kong and mainland China ~through the internet.~ Best Performance does not have any set-up or agents in mainland China. Upon conclusion of a sales transaction, Best Performance would ~instruct a third party logistics company to deliver~ the musical instruments from the Hong Kong warehouse to the customers’ locations.

The overseas branches of Best Performance also have close contacts with orchestras in different countries. Best Performance acts as an agent to help them arrange for performances in Hong Kong.

During the year ended 31 March 2017, Best Performance with the assistance of its UK branch arranged for the UK National Orchestras (“UNO”) to perform for three nights in Hong Kong City Hall in order to raise funds for the victims of the earthquake in Sichuan. Best Performance received subscription income and donations of HK$30 million and paid a fee of HK$600,000 to UNO. To meet its original commitment, Best Performance donated HK$5 million to the Sichuan local government for them to help the victims, and this donation was stated on their website. The rental and other charges for the event amounted to HK$500,000.
