# Profits tax - Example 75 - anti-avoidance IPRs
A Ltd is a toy trading company in Hong Kong. It commenced business in 2002 and closed its accounts annually on 30 June. In early 2011, A Ltd took over B Ltd, a company incorporated in the Mainland of China ('the Mainland'). After that, A Ltd and B Ltd reviewed their business processes, under which it was resolved that B Ltd would grant A Ltd a 10-year licence ('the Licence') to use B Ltd's trade mark ('the Trade mark') for selling toys in Hong Kong at a licence fee of RMB 10 million per year ('the Licence Fee') on 1 July 2011. B Ltd did not have any business presence in Hong Kong. The negotiation for and the decision to grant the Licence to A Ltd were made by the directors of B Ltd in the Mainland. On 1 December 2011, B Ltd sold the Trade mark with its right under the Licence at RMB 20 million to C Ltd, a Mainland corporation which is wholly-owned by A Ltd. On 1 January 2017, C Ltd terminated the Licence and sold the Trade mark to A Ltd at RMB50 million with an option for C Ltd to purchase back the Trade mark after five years. The market value of the Trade mark as at 1 January 2012 and 1 January 2017 were RMB 100 million and RMB150 million respectively.

Required

(a) Discuss whether and, if so, how B Ltd is chargeable to profits tax in respect of the Licence Fee.

(b) Advise whether your answer to (a) will be different if after the take-over, A Ltd and B Ltd shared the same group of directors who held all the board meetings in Hong Kong, and negotiated and concluded the agreement for the grant of the Licence in Hong Kong. Cite the relevant case law to support your answer.

(c) Determine whether A Ltd is entitled to a deduction for the purchase cost of the Trade mark for profits tax purposes.

Solution

(a) Whilst B Ltd did not carry on any business in Hong Kong, it received the Licence Fee from A Ltd in respect of the Trade mark used for selling toys in Hong Kong. By virtue of s.15(1)(b), the Licence Fee is deemed to arise in or be derived from Hong Kong from a trade, profession or business carried on by B Ltd in Hong Kong.

As it appears that the Trade mark had not been previously owned by any person carrying on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong, the assessable profits deemed to be derived from Hong Kong in respect of the Licence Fee should be computed as 30% of the Licence Fee pursuant to s.21A(1)(b)(ii). Since B Ltd is a non-resident, A Ltd is chargeable to profits tax on behalf of B Ltd in respect of the Licence Fee (s.20B(2)). (Taxation of non-residents is discussed in chapter 4 'Non-resident persons'.)

(b) If the directors of B Ltd held all the board meetings in Hong Kong, it is likely that B Ltd will be regarded as carrying on a business in Hong Kong (see De Beers Consolidated Mines Ltd v Howe [(1906) 5 TC 198]). Moreover, as B Ltd negotiated and concluded the agreement for the grant of the Licence in Hong Kong, the Licence Fee will also be regarded as having a source in Hong Kong (see CIR v HK-TVB International Limited (3 HKTC 468) and Lam Soon Trademark Limited v CIR (6 HKTC 768)). In the circumstances, B Ltd will be chargeable to profits tax in respect of the Licence Fee under s.14 instead of s.15(1)(b).

(c) Section16EA allows the deduction of expenditures incurred on the purchase of intellectual property even though they are capital in nature and should have been disallowed by virtue of s.17(1)(c). However, A Ltd is not entitled to any deduction under s.16EA because the relevant purchase cost is prohibited from deduction by virtue of ss.16EC(1) and (2), having regard to the following circumstances:

(i) The Trade mark had been used by A Ltd under the Licence of which the expiry date falls after 16 December 2011. Further, the Licence was terminated before its expiry and the Trade mark was purchased by A Ltd at a consideration below the market value with an option for licensor, C Ltd, to purchase back the Trade mark after five years. In such circumstances, it is likely that the Commissioner will consider that the consideration for the purchase of the Trade mark is not reasonable.

(ii) The Trade mark was purchased by A Ltd from its subsidiary, C Ltd.
