## 3.8 Cost and performance efficiency of different audit methodologies
As part of the planning process for any audit, the auditor must select the appropriate audit methodology to apply in the audit, in order to deliver the objectives of the audit in the most efficient manner possible. Possible methodologies are listed above.

Each methodology will incur costs and will offer **different levels of efficiency** in terms of the hours of work necessary to deliver the audit objectives. Remember that the auditor must decide on the appropriate blend of ~system testing~ and ~substantive procedures~, as in the diagram below.
![](Journal/Bear%20notes/Doc/Audit%20Methodologies/AA74D185-5901-48B2-9B56-20209A1E5F50.png)
Although an audit can be carried out with 100% substantive procedures, it is not possible to carry out a 100% systems audit. ~There must always be some substantive testing~ before an audit opinion can be delivered.

We shall look at each of the methodologies in turn.
	- 3.2 [[Risk-based audit]] - focused on high risk areas
	- 3.3 [[top-down approach]] - looking at the business and its objectives
	- 3.4 [[Systems audit versus system-based audit]] - focus on test of control
	- 3.5 [[Balance sheet approach]] - vouching account balance
	- 3.6 [[Transaction cycle approach]] - test a sample of transactions to make sure it process correctly throughout the cycle
	- 3.7 [[Directional testing]] - double entry
