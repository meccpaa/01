# Example: Settlement discounts
A company has annual credit sales of $30m with two months being the usual credit period given to customers. Not all customers, however, adhere to the policies as evidenced by the actual receivables ageing record as follows:

![](Example%20Settlement%20discounts/478EB302-AB5C-4359-BB76-27DBCB5AC0A4.png)

The company’s management decide to offer a 15% discount for payments made within 14 working days (assuming one year = 350 working days) of the invoice being raised and to reduce the maximum time allowed for payment to one month. It is estimated that 80% of customers will take the discount and the remainder will use the existing credit terms.

If such a scheme is offered to customers, sales will increase by 20% (at a contribution margin of 30%) and bad debts will be reduced from 10% to 6% of credit sales. The company requires a 25% per annum return on investment.

Is implementation of the early settlement discount scheme worthwhile in financial terms?
![](Example%20Settlement%20discounts/34B00BB2-94DF-4DF0-9B4D-036C022EE8CE.png)
The company should not adopt the early settlement discount scheme, as it results in a net decrease in benefits of $0.92 million.
