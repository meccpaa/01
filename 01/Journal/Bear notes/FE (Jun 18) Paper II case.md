# FE (Jun 18) Paper II: case
WT Holdings Ltd (“WT Holdings”) is a Cayman Islands incorporated company listed on the **Main Board** of the Stock Exchange in Hong Kong. The principal activities of WT Holdings and its subsidiaries (the “Group”) are providing **tour services in Hong Kong and overseas**. The Group has a financial year end of 31 December.

### WT Tours Ltd
WT Tours Ltd (“WT Tours”), with the relevant travel licence, is responsible for **organising tours** in and outside Hong Kong. It would pay an arm’s length marketing fee to WT Advertising Ltd (“WT Advertising”) for providing advertisement services. WT Tours also pays a ~cost plus 5% services fee~ for staff provided by WT Recruiting Ltd (“WT Recruiting”).

Customers are required to make full payment as [[deposits]] when they join the tours organised by WT Tours. During the year, a number of overseas tours were cancelled and part of the deposits received were not refunded to the customers. WT Tours was also subject to several litigations in relation to these overseas tours. The litigations related to ~non-compliance with laws and regulations~ but the management did ::not disclose:: the information related to the litigations in the notes to the financial statements. The potential exposures of some litigations were considered as **material**.

### WT Recruiting Ltd
WT Recruiting solely provides services to WT Tours and its profits are approximately 5% to its revenue. It recruits local as well as overseas employees or subcontractors to provide ~tour services~ in Hong Kong as well as overseas. 

### WT Advertising Ltd
WT Advertising, apart from providing ~advertising services~ to WT Tours, also ~sells advertising space~ on its website to hotels and airlines. The online advertising revenue includes **fixed** amounts determined based on the advertising period and **variable** amounts based on the number of clicks on the particular advertisement during the advertising periods. A ::system:: is developed by a third party IT company to capture the relevant information and calculate the amount for revenue recognition. After the first year of implementation, the system is now solely maintained by WT Advertising and any changes to the system or issues identified are handled by WT Advertising’s internal IT department.

WT Advertising also owns a unit in a **commercial building** in Central ( “Central property”), which is currently used as the ~office~ of the Group. It also owns a unit in an **industrial building** in Shatin (“Shatin property”) and currently ~leases~ it out to an independent third party for rental income. The lease term with tenants for Shatin property will end in the coming year.

The Group is currently considering not to renew the lease contracts with the tenant and to relocate its office from the Central property to the Shatin property. After relocation, the Central property will be leased out to earn rental income. In accordance with the accounting policy of the Group, property, plant and equipment are stated at cost less accumulated depreciation and any impairment losses while an investment property are stated at fair value.

The Group has engaged an **independent professional valuer** to perform valuation of the Shatin property and Central property on the date of change in use. The ~discounted cash flow method~ is used in estimating the fair value of the properties. [[Fair value]] A market-derived discount rate is applied to the projected cash flow in order to establish the present value of the income stream associated with the property. The duration of the cash flows and the specific timing of inflows and outflows are determined by events such as rent reviews, lease renewals, redevelopment or refurbishment. The periodic cash flow is estimated as gross income, non-recoverable expenses, lease incentives and other operating and management expenses. The series of periodic net operating income, along with an estimate of the terminal value anticipated at the end of the projection period, is then discounted. The significant inputs to the valuation include estimated future rental based on management’s estimation and discount rate.

The carrying value and the fair value of Central property on the date of change is HK$7,000,000 (net of impairment loss of HK$600,000) and HK$8,000,000, respectively. The **fair value** of the Shatin property on the date of change is HK$15,000,000.

### Share Option Scheme
The Group operates a share option scheme (the “Scheme”) to provide incentives and rewards to eligible participants, which includes directors (including non-executive directors), employees of the Group, suppliers and customers of the Group, who contribute to the success of the Group.

Currently the Group is considering granting 600,000 and 400,000 share options under the Scheme to certain directors and employees, respectively in the coming year and are considering three proposals as follows:

### Proposal A
The grant will be on the condition that they remain employed with the Group for the next two years from the grant date. The grantees will not be entitled to any options if they leave within two years after the grant date. They can exercise the options at any time between three and ten years from the grant date no matter whether they are with the Group or not . There are no cash settlement alternatives for the share options proposed to be granted.

### Proposal B
This proposal is similar to that of Proposal A except the grantees will be entitled to an extra 50% of the share options (i.e. 500,000 share options) if the share price is over HK$50 at the end of the two-year period after the grant date.

### Proposal C
The proposal is similar to that of Proposal A except the 1,000,000 share options granted will be vested equally in two tranches instead, i.e. 50% of share options (“Tranche 1 Options”) will be vested at the end of the first year (“Year 1”); and 50% of share options (“Tranche 2 Options”) will be vested at the end of the second year (“Year 2”). The vesting of the Tranche 1 Options and Tranche 2 Options are conditional on whether the directors and employees will remain in employment until the end of Year 1 and Year 2 respectively and certain revenue targets could be attained. The revenue targets for the Tranche 1 Options and Tranche 2 Options will be determined at the beginning of Year 1 and Year 2, respectively.

The Group do not expect any directors will leave the Group while 5% of the employees will leave the Group during the two-year period after the grant date and the terms of the share option awards do not contain any provision clause for employees who leave the Group before the end of the vesting period.

### Financing Strategy
The Chief Executive Officer (“CEO”) of the Group notices that the cash balance of WT Tours is HK$20,000,000 and contributes more than 50% of the total assets of WT Tours as at 31 December 2016. The CEO wonders if WT Tours could use a substantial amount of cash to update the IT system or purchase equipment to facilitate the daily work of staff. The CEO discusses the issue with the Chief Financial Officer ( “CFO”) and the Finance Director (“FD”). However, the CFO has a different view and addresses the importance of holding cash. The CEO suggests that WT Holdings can issue bonds or shares for raising capital if needed. The CEO plans to issue a 5% annual coupon bond with the coupon payment annually at the year end. The face value of the bond is HK$1,000 with ten years to maturity and the yield to maturity is 6%. To a certain extent, the FD agrees to issue bonds instead of shares to raise the capital. However, the FD suggests to the CEO that there are different choices for debt financing; and the choice of debt financing depends on many factors, such as, variation in market conditions. It may be difficult to issue long term bonds, convertible bonds or commercial papers due to the lack of investor demand.
