## 1. `HKAS 21` The Effects of Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates
HKAS 21 provides accounting guidance on the **translation** of [[foreign currency]] ~transactions~ and foreign currency ~financial statements~. It prescribes which [[8.1 exchange rates]] should be used and how [[exchange differences]] are recognised.

An entity may be involved in foreign activities in two ways:
(a) it may be involved in [[transactions in foreign currencies]], such as buying or selling goods.  (**Owe money or holds assets**)
(b) it may have [[foreign operations]]. 

HKAS 21 provides guidance on how foreign currency transactions should be translated and accounted for, and how the financial statements of foreign operations should be translated into a presentation currency prior to consolidation.

In particular, the standard considers:
- Which **exchange rate** to use, and 
- How **exchange differences** should be recognised in the financial statements.

## 2. Foreign currency transactions in individual company accounts
[[Foreign currency transactions]] are initially translated at the [[spot exchange rate]] on the transaction date. Balances relating to [[monetary items]] are re-translated at the reporting date. An exchange gain or loss may be recognised on **re-translation and settlement.**

## 3. Consolidation of foreign operations
Individual group entities must translate their financial statements into a common group [[presentation currency]] prior to consolidation by applying the method specified in HKAS 21.

Apart from the foreign currency aspects, the usual consolidation rules apply (intra-group transactions, non-controlling interest etc).

### Translation of financial statements to presentation currency
- Assets and liabilities — closing rate
- Income and expenses — spot rate
- Resulting exchange differences — OCI
- Share capital — historical rate
