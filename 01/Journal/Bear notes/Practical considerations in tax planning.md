# Practical considerations in tax planning
Effective tax planning should be the process of organising the affairs of a taxpayer in a legal and commercially realistic manner so as to reduce or defer the tax liability of the taxpayer.

### Example 12

A sale is made by Company A in Country A to Company B in Country B. Profit from the sale ($50) is taxed at, say, 30% in Country A. The tax liability of Company A will be $15.

![](Practical%20considerations%20in%20tax%20planning/4417672D-5221-4F0A-9AAC-214D65283121.png)
If the sale is first made by Company A to Company H in Hong Kong at $80 and then by Company H to Company B at $100, the overall tax liability can be reduced to $12.3 ($9 ($30 at 30%) in Country A + $3.3 ($20 at 16.5%) in Hong Kong).
- - - -
The above example is a typical transfer pricing arrangement. In order to reduce the amount of tax payable in Country A, more Hong Kong tax is paid rather than being avoided.

It should be noted that transfer pricing arrangements are being closely scrutinised by many countries. Rules on arm's length pricing have been established in many jurisdictions. These are outlined in section 6.5.6 on 'International transfer pricing'.
