# treasury self-test 1
What are the main difficulties in measuring the performance of a treasury department?
- - - -
Answer 1

A treasury department performs a variety of different functions, and it is not easy to identify an appropriate measure for all of them. For example, it is difficult to establish a performance measure for cash forecasting and cash management. Any measures that are used need to be relatively straight-forward to calculate eg error rates, average transaction cost.

It would be possible to establish the treasury department as a profit centre and measure it as such, if it had authority to speculate in the derivatives markets to make a profit. However there would be a risk that the department might give priority to its own profit-making activities rather than to the treasury needs of the rest of the company. Speculating in derivatives may also be risky and result in losses. In measuring the performance of treasury, risk should be considered as well as return.

Ultimately a treasury department should be assessed on its ability to provide the business units it supports with the right amount of funds, at the right time, in the right currency and at the right (market) price. Treasury should be adding value to other business units. It is difficult to find a performance measure which adequately reflects this and separating out the contribution of treasury to profits of other business units is likely to be challenging.

It may be possible to establish some performance measures for treasury by comparing actual performance against a target or market average. For example it may be possible to compare the company’s borrowing rates with current market rates, or average exchange rates on transactions in major currencies with average exchange rate for the period. Performance indicators could also be established for other facets of the treasury function against which its performance could be benchmarked, for example number of transactions processed, resources employed or total risk level.
