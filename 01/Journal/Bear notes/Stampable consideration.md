# Stampable consideration
Stamp duty is levied on the **value of consideration** which may be made up of money, money's worth, debts assigned or waived, securities, commodities or services performed. If the stated consideration is ~less than the market value~ of the immovable property or stock transferred, stamp duty is chargeable on the **market value**. Hence, the stampable value of:
	- (a) **immovable property** is based on the **market value** of the property free from ~any encumbrance~; and
	- (b) **stock** is based on the **net asset value** of the stock determined with reference to the latest accounts of the company and market value of the assets at the time of transfer.

If there is ::no stated consideration::, the instrument is regarded as a ~voluntary disposition inter vivos~ (i.e. a gift made during lifetime), and stamp duty is chargeable on the **market value** of the immovable property or stock transferred (see section 7 on 'Voluntary disposition inter vivos').

## Consideration consisting of stock
Pursuant to s.22(1), where the consideration or any part of the consideration for a COS consists of any stock, stamp duty is chargeable by reference to the **value of the stock** ~on the date of conveyance or transfer~.

## Consideration consisting of security not being stock
Pursuant to s.22(2), where the consideration or any part of the consideration for a COS consists of any security which is not stock, stamp duty is chargeable by reference to ~the amount due on the date of conveyance or transfer~ for the **principal and interest** upon the security.

## Consideration consisting of debts payable by the transferee
SS.24(1) provides that debts, including contingent ones, payable by the transferee are treated as **part of the consideration** for a conveyance of immovable property or transfer of stock that is chargeable to stamp duty.

## Consideration in the form of debts waived
Under s.24(1), debts waived by the transferee are treated as **part of the consideration** for a conveyance of immovable property or transfer of stock. If the debt consideration exceeds the value of the property conveyed or stock transferred, stamp duty is chargeable based on the higher amount of the debt to be settled by the immovable property or stock. However, pursuant to s.24(2), the person liable for the stamp duty may apply to have the stamp duty payable based on the ~lower value of the immovable property or stock~. In such case, the chargeable instrument (the conveyance or contract notes) has to be ::adjudicated:: (see section 9.5 on 'Adjudication').

## Consideration in the form of debts assigned
Pursuant to s.24(3), when there is a **transfer of a beneficial interest** in a body corporate and the transferee will incur liability in respect of any **indebtedness** of the body corporate, the amount of the indebtedness will form part of the consideration for the transfer.

## Consideration expressed in foreign currency
For the purpose of calculating the stampable value, the consideration is translated into Hong Kong dollars at the rate of exchange prevailing on the date of the instrument. As defined in s.18(2), the 'rate of exchange' means the buying rate for the currency in question, as determined by the Monetary Authority, for telegraphic transfers at the commencement of business on the date of the instrument or, if that date is a Sunday or a general holiday, on the business day immediately preceding that date.

## Contingency principle
Stamp duty is assessed on the basis of the circumstances prevailing at the time an instrument is executed. Liability to stamp duty cannot be altered or affected by events subsequent to the execution. This so-called 'contingency principle' applies as follows:
(a) If the consideration under an instrument is unascertainable at the time of execution, no stamp duty can be assessed.
(b) If the consideration is uncertain but the instrument contains a clause on the amounts of maximum and minimum consideration payable, stamp duty is assessed on the maximum amount payable. Any duty paid will not be refunded even though the consideration payable at a later date is less than the maximum amount stated.
(c) If the consideration is uncertain but the instrument contains a clause on the amount of minimum consideration payable, stamp duty is assessed on the minimum amount payable. Further duty cannot be assessed even though the consideration payable at a later date is more than the minimum amount stated.
