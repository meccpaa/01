# FE Dec 19 Paper II Section B - 6 foreign tax credit
**In relation to the recently discovered tax receipt issued by the Indonesian tax authority, advise Thomas on the actions and time limit the Company can take to claim full relief of the foreign tax credit with respect to the withholding tax paid in Indonesia.**
**Your advice should include a discussion of the relevant provision(s) of the Inland Revenue Ordinance.**
**(6 marks)**
- - - -
## Answer
According to Article 22 of the double taxation agreement (“DTA”) between Hong Kong and Indonesia, where the income of a Hong Kong resident is subject to tax in both Hong Kong and Indonesia, the Hong Kong resident can credit the tax paid in Indonesia on the relevant income against the Hong Kong tax liability arising on the same income. 
As the royalty income was subject to Hong Kong profits tax in the year of assessment 2016/17, the Indonesia withholding tax paid would only be allowed as a tax credit for the year of assessment 2016/17. 
However, the time limit for making a foreign tax credit claim provided in s.50(9) is within 2 years after the end of the relevant year of assessment, i.e., 31 March 2019, has already passed. 
The extended six-year time limit only applies to tax payable for the year of assessment beginning on or after 1 April 2018 (s.2 of Schedule 44 *Transitional Provisions for Inland Revenue (Amendment) (No.6) Ordinance 2018*). 
Nonetheless, the Company could still lodge a claim for foreign tax credit and explained to the IRD why it had failed to lodge the foreign tax credit claim in time. The IRD has expressed in its annual meeting with the HKICPA in 2010 that it agrees with the OECD that double taxation relief should always be granted whenever possible. 
The Indonesian withholding tax paid is a tax on income, which is an appropriation of profits. Therefore, the withholding tax is not an outgoing or expense allowable for deduction under s.16(1) of the IRO. 
However, the Company could not claim the foreign tax credit under s.70A, which observes a six-year limit. This is because the issue of foreign tax credit claim had been specifically dealt with under s.50(9). Furthermore, s.70A only empowers an assessor to correct an “assessment” and the tax charged thereon, but not the amount of tax payable (per 2010 Annual Meeting between the IRD and the HKICPA, agenda item A4(b)).

## Panel
This question asked candidates whether the Company could now claim a tax credit in respect of a royalty income which has been subject to double taxation in Hong Kong and Indonesia in 2016/17. 
Candidates’ performance was disappointing. Many candidates were not aware that there is a specific provision in s.50(9) governing the claiming of tax credit. Instead, they said that the Company could rely on s.70A to re-open the 2016/17 year of assessment to claim the tax credit. 
For those candidates that were able to point out that the Company could rely on s.50(9) to claim the tax credit, they were not aware that the recently extended 6-year time limit for claiming tax credit only apply to tax payable on or after 1 April 2018. As the withholding tax in question concerned with tax paid in the year of assessment 2016/17, the previous 2-year time limit would apply. That means, the time limit for applying tax credit has lapsed. 
Nonetheless, the Company could still lodge a claim for tax credit and explained to the IRD why it had failed to lodge the claim in time. The IRD has expressed in its annual meeting with the HKICPA that double taxation relief should always be granted whenever possible.
