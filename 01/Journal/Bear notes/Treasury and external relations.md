# Treasury and external relations
Treasury has a number of relationships which it needs to carefully manage in order to operate effectively. In some cases, such as the organisation’s bankers, the relationship will be principally managed by treasury. In other cases, the relationship will be managed in conjunction with other functions within the organisation or in conjunction with advisers.

## 11.1 The banker relationship

A company’s relationship with bankers has undergone significant transformation over the past twenty years due to vastly increased competition in the banking and financial services industry. This is even more pronounced today in view of the recent global financial crisis and its effects on companies.

Banks now operate with higher regulatory capital requirements to guard against the risk of failure. They are also more careful to avoiding concentration risk through lending to one particular customer or industry sector. Therefore treasurers may face a higher cost of borrowing and banks have become more discriminating in their choice of lending. As a result treasurers need to develop strong relationships with banks to ensure that they can obtain stable and secure sources of funding. Tresurers may wish to diversify the number of banks they deal with so that their ability to borrow may not be compromised.

Banking relationships have a key role to play for a company to achieve an effective and efficient treasury operation. Cash management (all transactional activities both domestic and international) is the area directly affected by banking processes. In addition, funding, availability of appropriate treasury risk management products and specialist services also involve banks.

Treasurers may be wise to review periodically the service provided by their bankers. They may wish to standardise and streamline their banking processes (such as payment outsourcing) by consolidating multiple bank relations into a few so that their company has more bargaining power with its banks.

A successful banker relationship is dependent on objective advice, regular contact, rapid problem resolution, fair compensation and the bank's operational capabilities. Treasurers have to ensure that they have the flexibility to switch banks by having flexibility in their treasury operations and systems. Excessive reliance on systems and resources provided by a company’s bankers may make switching banks more difficult.

The banking relationship must be a two-way process. Corporate treasurers expect to receive objective advice from their bankers in all areas covering the provision of financial services and vice versa. Any problems being encountered by the company should be discussed openly and honestly with bankers, especially when a borrowing situation exists and in difficult economic times. Neither the company nor the banker wants any surprises in their relationship with each other.

## 11.2 Other relationships

In addition to banks. treasury operations involve regular interactions with other external organisation with whom an effective relationship can be developed. These include:

- Investors: Treasurers need to be aware of the requirements of other external sources of finance such as equity investors and debt investors. For unlisted companies, depending on the stage of the company’s development, potential equity investors include venture capital and private equity as well as existing shareholders. In the case of listed companies the pool of potential equity investors widens to include individual investors and fund managers representing the interests of institutional investors.

Although treasury needs to have an understanding of the structure of long term funding and the cost of equity finance, it is unlikely to be the principal function responsible for the company’s relationship with current and potential shareholders. In the case of unlisted companies, company management and the finance department are typically the principal points of contact with sources of equity funding, such as venture capital and private equity. In the case of listed companies the relationship with shareholders will be the responsibility of an investor relations function which may be internal or external. In addition an investment bank will be retained in order to advise on new equity issues. Similarly, the corporate relationship with potential debt investors is likely to be managed in conjunction with external advisers.

- Rating agencies: The credit rating provided for a company by credit agencies such as Moody’s or Standard and Poor’s have an impact on the availability and cost of finance for that company. The higher a company’s credit rating the lower the credit risk it represents to a lender and the lower the interest rate charged as a consequence. Obtaining a credit rating is also likely to streamline the borrowing process and the lender(s) are able to rely on the credit risk assessment of the independent credit rating agency.

As discussed in Chapter 9 the process of obtaining a credit rating involves extensive interaction between the company and the agency’s analytical team to assist in providing the necessary information for the agency to make a rating decision. This will typically include a presentation by senior management, site visits as well as answering analyst queries. This rating is communicated to the company, providing an opportunity for comment and feedback from the company before the rating is disseminated.

Once rated the credit rating agency will carry out on going surveillance of the company to determine whether the rating needs to be adjusted. In this process the agency may request financial information from the company.

Treasury may have a role in managing this process and the relationship with the credit rating agency. This could include the preparation of information for presentations and responding to questions from members of the agency’s analytical team.

- Regulators: In their activities in the financial markets treasury operations may be required to communicate with financial regulators such as the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and the Securities and Futures Commission of Hong Kong. If they are operating in foreign financial markets corporate treasury centres may also need to interact with the regulatory authorities of other countries.

Companies listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange also have obligations under the rules of the exchange. For example these rules include requirements related to transactions with connected persons which could be applicable to some treasury transactions with other group members. Corporate treasury centres will need to be familiar with these requirements to ensure that the necessary procedures are followed for any transaction conducted falling under the scope of these rules. This may on occasion require treasury to interact with the exchange to clarify the interpretation of its rules.
