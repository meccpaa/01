# FE Dec 19 Paper II Section B - 5(b) severance payment - salaries tax
**(b)  The salaries tax implication for the dismissed employees of the severance payments received.**
**(6 marks)**
- - - -
## Answer
Income from an employment located in Hong Kong is liable to salaries tax under s.8(1)(a) and s.9(1)(a). As such, termination payments that are made in recognition of past services is taxable. 
As a matter of practice, the IRD has always accepted statutory severance payment computed pursuant to the EO as not chargeable to salaries tax, as it is not an emolument from employment. As reflected in the legislative intent of enacting the bill providing severance payment, it is in the nature of compensation to relieve an employee for the hardships suffered as a result of the termination of employment. 
The IRD’s view is supported by the case *Mairs v Haughey [1994] 1 AC 303*, which held that severance payment in its nature is not an emolument from employment. 
However, the excess portion over the statutory amount may be chargeable to salaries tax, if the excess amount was in fact reward for services rendered in past services. In this regard, one needs to look at all the circumstances under which the excessive amount was paid by the employer to ascertain whether it was made in connection with services rendered by the employee and thus taxable. 
On the basis that the excess portion of the severance payment over the EO was not provided for in the employment contracts, and given there is sufficient documentary proof of CHL’s intention to relieve hardship of the dismissed employees, the excess portion should not be regarded as a reward for past services. As such, the entire amount of severance payment that was received in the hands of the dismissed employees should not be assessable to salaries tax.

## Panel
This question related to the taxability of severance payment in the hands of the employees. 
The candidates’ performance was fair. Many candidates were able to analyse that, same as profits tax, they need to segregate the portion paid in accordance to EO and the portion in excess of the EO. 
While most of them were able to correctly point out that the portion of payments paid in accordance to EO is not taxable, they could not cite the relevant legal principles. 
For the portion in excess of the EO, a good number of candidates were able to relate from the background facts that the excess portion was made to relieve hardship of the middle-aged employees and thus, not chargeable to salaries tax. However, many candidates justified their conclusion by citing advance ruling case no.25. Candidates are minded that advance ruling cases are published by the IRD for general reference only. Given the limited facts provided in an advance ruling case, it should not be regarded as case law authority.
