# FE (Jun 18) Paper I: Section B (Q7 sales HK/China)
[[FE (Jun 18) Paper I: case]]

Question 7 (13 marks – approximately 23 minutes)

(a) For those sales to Chinese customers made by Best Performance, analyse whether they are subject to Hong Kong and / or Mainland taxes. If yes, state the type of tax and the respective rate(s). (5 marks)

(b) In relation to the performance made by UNO in Hong Kong, advise whether there is any withholding obligation for Best Performance. Calculate the amount to be withheld, if any. State the relevant provision. (4 marks)

(c) Analyse whether the amount paid by Best Performance to the Sichuan local government would qualify as a tax deductible charitable donation. If not, consider and explain any other possible argument by which the amount could be claimed as tax deductible under Hong Kong Profits Tax. (4 marks)
- - - -
[[Chargeability of e-commerce transaction]]

## Answer 7(a)
The sales to Chinese customers would be regarded by the Inland Revenue Department (“IRD”) as sourced in Hong Kong and subject to Hong Kong profits tax at 16.5% because the subject profit is a **trading** one [[Source of Trading Profits]] and the **sale contracts were effected** [[Contract effected test]] in **Hong Kong**, notwithstanding that the ~purchase contracts~ were effected ::outside Hong Kong:: through overseas branches.

[[DIPN 21 (Revised)]]
According to the Departmental Interpretation and Practice Notes (“DIPN”) No.21 – Locality of profits, if **either** the purchase or sale contract is effected in Hong Kong, the IRD will regard the related trading profit as ~sourced in Hong Kong~. Depending on the specific facts of the case, Best Performance may raise the argument that the ::importance of purchase contracts:: outweighed the sales contracts according to the tax case of (Magna Industrial Co Ltd v CIR), thus the trading profits should be offshore sourced and non-taxable. [[Magna Industrial Co Ltd (1997) 1 HKRC 90–082]]

The sales to Chinese customers should not be subject to Corporate Income Tax in the Mainland because Best Performance does not have any PE in China. However, the sales to Chinese customers should still be subject to VAT at 17% upon importation.

## Answer 7(b)
Under s.20B, the Hong Kong payer shall withhold an amount sufficient for the tax payable by the non-resident performers in Hong Kong. [[s.20B - withholding obligations on resident persons paying or crediting certain payments to non-resident persons]]

According to DIPN No.17, one-third of the income is allowed as deductible expenses.

In other words, Best Performance would have to withhold HK$60,000 [(HK$600,000 x 2 / 3) x 15%] in relation to the HK$600,000 fee payable to UNO.

## Answer 7(c)
Sichuan local government is an ::overseas entity:: and is unlikely to be a ~Hong Kong approved charitable institution~, so the amount paid to it would not qualify as a tax deductible charitable donation under [[s.16D Approved charitable donations ('ACDs’)]]

Nevertheless, Best Performance may still claim such amount as deductible under s.16(1) on the ground that the amount spent served ~promotion purposes~ and hence it could be viewed as expenses incurred in generating the assessable profits of the company. [[s.16 General Deductions]]
