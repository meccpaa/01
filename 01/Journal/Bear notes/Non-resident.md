## Tax implications of residence and non-residence
Although residence is not a criterion upon which taxes under the IRO are imposed, there are special provisions relating to non-resident persons, especially for recovery purposes.
- - - -
As Hong Kong adopts a [[territorial basis]], the concept of residence is of **little importance** except for isolated purposes such as 
	- the eligibility to elect for [[Personal Assessment]] (see s.41(4)), 
	- business with non-resident persons and 
	- the exemptions available to [[Determining the central management and control]]. [[Non-resident]]

There are no definitions for 'residents' and 'non-residents' under the IRO except in the case of aircraft owners (see ss.23C and 23D), offshore funds and offshore private equity funds (see s.20AB(3)). 
	- For the purpose of ss.20A and 20B, the IRD has accepted that a 'non-resident' is a person who has no permanent business presence in Hong Kong (DIPN 17 (Revised)). Pursuant to IRR 5, [[permanent establishment]] means `a branch, management or other place of business, but does not include an agent unless the agent has, and habitually exercises, a general authority to negotiate and conclude contracts on behalf of his principal or has a stock of merchandise from which he regularly fills orders on his behalf`. 
		- Having no permanent establishment in Hong Kong does not mean that the non-resident person is exempt from tax in Hong Kong. 
		- A non-resident person may be liable to [[Property tax]], [[Salaries Tax]]  or [[Profit tax]] if he has ~income arising in or derived from Hong Kong~ falling within the **scope of charge** of these three taxes. The **same rates** of tax apply to both resident persons and non-resident persons.
