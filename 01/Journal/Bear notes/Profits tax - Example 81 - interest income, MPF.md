# Profits tax - Example 81 - interest income, MPF
Synergy Santa Ltd ('Synergy') is a company established in Hong Kong engaging in the investment holding business. The following incomes and expenditures have been credited and charged respectively to its financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2017.

(1) Interest income

Bank interest income of $35,000 has been derived from a Hong Kong dollar fixed deposit placed with a local bank. The deposit was pledged to guarantee an overdraft banking facility provided by the same bank to Synergy. During the year, Synergy did not utilise any of the facility and only incurred bank charges of $500 as an overdraft facility renewal expense.

(2) Waiver of loan borrowed from a shareholder

A loan of $2,000,000 has been borrowed by Synergy from one of its shareholders in the year 2010. The loan was utilised by Synergy as working capital for its daily business operations, and has been stated in the financial statements of Synergy in relevant years as a long-term liability. In a recent meeting amongst the shareholders of Synergy, the respective shareholder agreed to waive Synergy from the requirement to repay the loan.

(3) Contributions to Mandatory Provident Fund ('MPF')

Synergy made an ordinary annual contribution of $630,000. The amount was calculated based on 18% of each employee's remuneration in line with the company's policy. In addition, Synergy also made a special contribution of $390,000 to its MPF scheme on top of the ordinary annual contribution.

Required

(a) Discuss the taxability of the following incomes of Synergy for the year. Put forward your analysis from the views of Synergy and the IRD where applicable.

(i) Interest income

(ii) Waiver of loan borrowed from a shareholder

(b) Discuss the deductibility of the MPF contributions made by Synergy for the year and, where applicable, compute the amount of allowable deduction for Synergy.

Solution

(a) (i)

Synergy may claim the bank interest income as exempt from profits tax under the Exemption from Profits Tax (Interest Income) Order 1998 ('the Order') on the basis that the interest income was derived from a local bank, and there was no interest expenses deductible under ss.16(1)(a), 16(2)(c), (d) or (e). However, under the Order, the exemption shall not apply in the case of any deposit which is used to secure or guarantee money borrowed referred to in s.16(1)(a) where the condition for the application of s.16(1)(a) is satisfied under ss.16(2)(c), (d) or (e) and s.16(2A) does not apply. In this regard, the exemption claim may be challenged on the grounds that the availability of the overdraft facility and the respective bank charges have satisfied the condition for the application of s.16(1)(a), and accordingly the exemption does not apply to Synergy. Whether interest expense deduction has ever been claimed under ss.16(2)(c), (d) or (e) is irrelevant to the exemption claim.

(ii) Synergy could argue that the income derived from the waiver of loan should not be subject to profits tax under s.14(1) on the grounds that (i) the amount is capital in nature as it was derived from a waiver of long-term loan advanced from a shareholder, and (ii) the amount is not derived from any of its recurring business on trading activities. However, the IRD may apply s.15(1)(c) to deem the amount as a taxable trading receipt. Specifically the amount would be regarded as a grant, subsidy or financial assistance to Synergy in connection with the carrying on of its business, as the loan was utilised as working capital for its daily business operations.

(b) MPF ordinary annual contribution $630,000 – MPF annual contribution incurred in the production of taxable income is deductible under s.16(1), except that the maximum deductible amount is limited to 15% of the total emoluments of each employee under s.17(1)(h). In this regard, the deductible amount of MPF ordinary annual contribution of Synergy should be $525,000 ($630,000 ÷ 18% × 15%).

MPF Special Contribution $390,000 – The special contribution to MPF is capital in nature and therefore non-deductible under s.17(1)(c). However, under s.16A, the amount is deductible over five years in equal annual installments. In this regard, the deductible amount is $78,000 for the year ($390,000 × 1/5).
