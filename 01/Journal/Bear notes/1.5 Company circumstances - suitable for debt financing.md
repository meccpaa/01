# 1.5 Company circumstances - suitable for debt financing
One determinant of the suitability of the gearing mix is the stability of the company. It may seem obvious, but it is worth stressing that debt financing will be more appropriate in the following circumstances:

(a) The company is in a healthy competitive position.
(b) Cash flows and earnings are stable.
(c) Profit margins are reasonable.
(d) Operational gearing is low (i.e. the fixed costs that have to be covered by profits from trading activities are low).
(e) The bulk of the company's assets are tangible.
(f) The liquidity and cash flow position is strong.
(g) The debt-equity ratio is low.
(h) Share prices are low.

High financial gearing may arise in heavy manufacturing where the company needs to borrow to invest in long life, high cost plant and machinery.

Service industries and retail businesses will typically have lower financial gearing – supermarkets for example tend to generate a large amount of cash from customers before they have to pay suppliers, so their need for external debt finance is reduced.

A financial gearing ratio of 70% may be acceptable for a stable business such as a utility company, but not for a risky technology company. Companies developing new risky activities e.g. bio-research or investing in unstable or emerging overseas markets are more likely to raise finance through equity than debt.

In general, a company with a **high gearing ratio** (high operating gearing and high financial gearing) may be **more vulnerable to economic downturns**. This is because it has to cover fixed operating costs, make interest payments and service its debt through cash flows that may be significantly lower due to the downturn.
