# s.70A error or omission claim
An assessment that has become **final and conclusive** may still be ~re-opened~ by an application under s.70A of the IRO.

The **conditions** under s.70A are as follows:
- It is established to the satisfaction of an assessor that the assessment is **excessive** due to an ~error or omission~ in a return or statement or that there is an arithmetical error or omission in the calculation of the assessable income or profits or tax charged; and
- An application is made ~within six years~ after the end of the year of assessment **or** within ~six months after the date on which the Notice of Assessment~ was served, whichever is the **later**.

However, ::no correction:: will be made under s.70A if the assessment was issued in ~accordance with the **practice generally prevailing** at the time the return or statement was made~. Also, s.70A cannot be invoked to re-open an estimated assessment if it was issued **in the absence of a return**. Refer to Sun Yau Investment Co. Ltd.

What constitutes a **prevailing practice** is a question of fact. When there is a legal decision made in favour of a taxpayer, assessments which became final and conclusive before that legal decision should not be re-opened under s.70A.

If an assessor refuses to correct an assessment under s.70A, the taxpayer may object to such notice of refusal in the same manner as an objection against a Notice of Assessment.
