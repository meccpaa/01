# Example: ROI versus residual income
Suppose that Department H has the following profit, assets employed and an imputed interest charge of 12% on operating assets:

![](Example%20ROI%20versus%20residual%20income/AF3C000C-C95C-4486-B4D4-0C503A35581D.png)

Suppose now that an additional investment of $10,000 is proposed, which will increase operating income in Department H by $1,400. The effect of the investment would be:
![](Example%20ROI%20versus%20residual%20income/98CFB61B-852D-4AA0-BFAD-3FE396009F69.png)
If the Department H manager is made responsible for the department's performance, he would resist the new investment if he were to be judged on ROI, but would welcome the investment if he were judged according to RI, since there would be a marginal increase of $200 in residual income from the investment, but a fall of 1.5% in ROI.

The marginal investment offers a return of 14% ($1,400 on an investment of $10,000) which is above the 'cut-off rate' of 12%. Since the original return on investment was 30%, the marginal investment will reduce the overall divisional performance. Indeed, any marginal investment offering an accounting rate of return of less than 30% in the year would reduce the overall performance.
