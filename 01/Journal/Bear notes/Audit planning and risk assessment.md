## audit planning
Auditors must plan their work so that it is undertaken in an effective manner. The auditors formulate an overall audit strategy which is translated into a detailed audit plan for audit staff to follow.

1. [[Audit Planning - HKSA 300]] - An **effective and efficient audit** relies on proper planning procedures. The planning process is covered in general terms by HKSA 300 Planning an Audit of Financial Statements which states that the objective of the auditor is to plan the audit so that the engagement is performed in an effective manner.
2. [[Understanding the entity and its environment - HKSA 315]] - The auditor is required to obtain an understanding of the entity and its environment in order to be **able to assess** the risks of material misstatement.
3. [[Materiality HKSA 320, HKSA 450]] - Assessing whether an omission or misstatement may influence the **decision-making** by users, requires consideration of the characteristics of those users and how the information may be used. (Planning stages, and reviewed throughout the audit)
4. [[Risk Assessment]] - When the auditor has obtained an understanding of the entity, he shall identify [[significant risks]] and assess the risks of material misstatement in the financial statements.
5. [[Overall responses to assessed RoMM - HKSA 330]] - The overall audit strategy and detailed audit plan may need to be **revised** to address the assessed risk of material misstatement.
