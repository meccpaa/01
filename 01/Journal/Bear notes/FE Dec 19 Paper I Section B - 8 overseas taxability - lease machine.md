# FE Dec 19 Paper I Section B - 8 overseas taxability - lease machine
Ms Rose Cheng, Finance Director of the Group, noted that the relevant terms of the lease agreement with PII with respect to the 3D scanning and 3D printing machines stipulated that the consideration of US$500,000 is exclusive of any tax(es) applied on such rental income under the laws of Hong Kong. MPHK shall pay when due, either directly or as reimbursement to PII, the applicable taxes on the rental income. 
**Required:**
**As the tax advisor of the Group, write a memo to advise Rose the Hong Kong tax implications and reporting obligations for the year of assessment 2017/18, if any, with respect to the lease agreement with PII. Specifically, Rose would like to understand whether PII would be liable to Hong Kong Profits Tax (as any of such tax would be borne by MPHK), and if so, the basis of how the tax liability is to be ascertained, and how should MPHK or PII report such tax to the Inland Revenue Department.**
**You are not required to compute the exact tax lability (if any), and discuss the deductibility of the lease payment to PII.**
Marks will be awarded for appropriateness of the form and presentation of the memo.

## Subject:  Hong Kong tax implications and reporting obligations in respect of the lease agreement with PII 

We refer to your enquiry in relation to the rental payments made to PII with respect to the 3D scanning and 3D printing machines. As requested, we provide below our comments on the respective issues. 

### Taxability of rental income received by PII 
While PII has no other business presence in Hong Kong, and thus not chargeable to Hong Kong profits tax under s.14, it is deemed to be chargeable to tax under s.15(1)(d), as it received rental income in respect of the 3D scanning and 3D printing machines used by MPHK in Hong Kong. 
S.21A does not apply in fixing the assessable profit in respect of a sum deemed chargeable under s.15(1)(d). As such, **the normal rules apply and the assessable profit is the gross amount of rental receipt, less allowable expenses and depreciation allowances**. The applicable tax rate is 16.5%. 
`Article 12 Royalties of the DTA` between mainland China and Hong Kong provides that the term “royalties” is defined to include payment received as a consideration for the use of industrial or commercial equipment. The tax so charged is 7% of the gross amount of rental receipt. 
Under DIPN 44, the IRD states that Hong Kong adopts the` “preferential treatment” principle`. Where the DTA and IRO contain different provisions relating to the same matter, preference will be given to the provisions that are most **beneficial** to the taxpayers. 
That means the tax payable on the rental receipt would be the lower of the sum computed under s.15(1)(d) and the DTA. 

### Reporting obligations 
MPHK has no obligations to withhold the tax from the rental payments it made to PII as it was not an agent of PII. MPHK and PII deal with each other on a principal-to-principal basis. Furthermore, sums deemed chargeable under s.15(1)(d) are not covered in s.20B, under which a Hong Kong payer is required to withhold the tax payable in respect of certain sums for a non-resident person. 
::MPHK did not have any obligation to report:: to the IRD in the year of assessment 2017/18 because the rental payments were made during MPHK’s financial year ended 31 December 2018. MPHK is only required to report the rental payments made to PII in the relevant box in its 2018/19 profits tax return. 
Please feel free to let us know if you have any questions regarding the above. 
Best regards, [Tax advisor]

## Penal
This question required candidates to explain the profits tax implications in respect of machinery rental payments made to a mainland Chinese company, and how such payments would interact with Article 12 Royalties of the DTA between mainland China and Hong Kong. 

The candidates’ performance was poor. While most candidates correctly pointed out that the rental income received by PII, which has no business presence in Hong Kong, is deemed to be chargeable to tax under s.15(1)(d), they ~did not know how the tax liabilities were to be ascertained and MPHK’s obligations under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (“IRO”)~. They gave their answers on the misunderstanding that the tax implications in respect of royalties paid to a non-resident equally apply to rental payments made to a non-resident. 

Most candidates were also not aware that Article 12 Royalties of the DTA between mainland China and Hong Kong **cover payment received as a consideration** for the use of industrial or commercial equipment, and how it would operate to ~reduce the profits tax liabilities~ of PII in Hong Kong. 

In addition, many candidates did not realize that MPHK has no obligations to withhold the tax from the rental payments it made to PII under the IRO. This is the case as s.20B did not apply to sums deemed chargeable under s.15(1)(d), and **MPHK was not an agent** of PII as they dealt with each other on a principal-to-principal basis.
