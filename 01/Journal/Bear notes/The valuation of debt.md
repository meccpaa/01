# The valuation of debt
Models such as the free cash flow to the firm (FCFF) and the DCF approach arrive at a value for the total company (i.e. enterprise value) rather than just the equity stake.

As discussed above, in order to arrive at the value of equity, the value of other claims such as debt and preference shares must be deducted.

The concepts used to value debt were introduced in Chapter 9, which considered the calculation of the cost of debt capital. The relevant formula for deriving the market value of different forms of debt are summarised here.

## 7.2 Redeemable debt
Key term

The valuation of redeemable debt depends on future expected receipts. The market value is the discounted present value of future interest receivable, up to the year of redemption, plus the discounted present value of the redemption payment.

![](The%20valuation%20of%20debt/1603612D-79FD-40AA-89D9-972780722B2F.png)
Value of debt = (Interest earnings x Annuity factor) + (Redemption value x Discounted cash flow factor)

P n = redemption value of the bond at maturity (often par value of the bond)
