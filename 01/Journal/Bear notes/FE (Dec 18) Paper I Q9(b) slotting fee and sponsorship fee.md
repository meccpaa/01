# FE (Dec 18) Paper I: Q9(b) slotting fee and sponsorship fee
Referring to Question 1, elaborate on the tax treatment of the slotting fee from the Group’s perspective. For the sponsorship fee income to the distributors, elaborate on the tax treatment from the distributors’ perspective. You should explain with supporting tax principles.
(14 marks)
[[FE (Dec 18) Paper I: Q1 sales incentive]]
- - - -
## Solution
### Slotting fee from the Group’s perspective
For the Slotting fee, the issue is whether the amount is a [[capital expenditure]] or not. If it is a capital expenditure, the amount or its amortisation are both not deductible.

Whether the Slotting fee is a capital expenditure in turn depends on a number of tests such as “the **enduring benefits** test”, the “**once and for all** test” and “**profit-yielding structure** test”, see the Wharf Properties case.
[[Capital vs Revenue nature]]

A position could be taken that a 3-year benefit is not “an enduring benefit” and the Slotting fee is a recurring expenditure, therefore it is revenue in nature and is deductible following the “tax follows accounting” principle under the Secan case.

There is no apparent contradiction to the tax principle as the amount in question is not unrealised profits or specific provision of stock loss under the Nice Cheer case.

### Sponsorship fee income from the distributor’s perspective
Regarding the sponsorship fee, the amount, in general, is ::not taxable:: whether under s.14 or s.15(1)(c) because it is connected with capital expenditure. [[s.15(1)(c) - grants]]

The distributors’ **capital expenditure** on design and decoration should be ::correspondingly reduced:: by the sponsorship fee. The ~related [[Depreciation allowances]]~ and ~commercial building annual allowance~ should then be reduced.

If the income is **not offset** against the distributors’ capital expenditure on design and decoration, and reported separately as an income item, the sponsorship income may be **regarded as an ordinary income** received from the distributors’ business. As such it is taxable under s.14.

If the **full or partial amount of sponsorship fee is** ::refunded:: back to the Group by the distributors due to the failure to meet the minimum quantity, such amount to the distributors is an ordinary expense incurred in generating assessable profits and should be **deductible** under S16(1) of the IRO. Alternatively, if the payment pertains to the income used to reduce capital expenditure, the ~corresponding capital expenditure and related depreciation allowances should be increased accordingly~.
