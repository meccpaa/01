# new Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622)
The Hong Kong new Companies Ordinance forms the core of the regulation of companies in Hong Kong. It sets out the rules and regulations governing Hong Kong companies and the conduct of their affairs.

A new Companies Ordinance and the subsidiary legislation came into effect on 3 March 2014. At the same time the core provisions affecting the operation of companies in the previous Companies Ordinance (Cap. 32) were repealed. However provisions relating to prospectuses, winding-up, insolvency of companies and disqualification were not repealed and the provisions of the old ordinance (Cap. 32) remain in force on these areas. Accordingly the old ordinance was retitled as Companies (Winding Up and Miscellaneous Provisions) Ordinance (Cap. 32).

The **Companies Registry** ~administers and enforces~ most parts of the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance. It registers local and non-Hong Kong companies and statutory returns, and makes available for public inspection company information kept by the Registry.

The new Companies Ordinance applies to all companies formed and registered under Companies Ordinance. It is not limited just to listed companies, but applies also to unlisted public and private companies. The new Companies Ordinance is now the main law that applies to companies registered in Hong Kong, and is one of the largest and most complex pieces of legislation in Hong Kong.

The new Companies Ordinance (Cap.622), which consists of 921 sections and 11 schedules, provides a modernised legal framework for the incorporation and operation of companies in Hong Kong. It aims to achieve four main objectives, namely, to enhance corporate governance; ensure better regulation; facilitate business; and modernise the law.
