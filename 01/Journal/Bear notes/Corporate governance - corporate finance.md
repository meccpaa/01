# Corporate governance - corporate finance
Corporate governance refers to the rules, systems and processes by which a company is directed and controlled. A variety of stakeholder groups have the power to influence a company's policies. It is the directors' duty to balance the conflicting demands of these stakeholders, while at the same time preserving the interests of the shareholders.

Corporate governance refers to the rules, systems and processes by which a company is directed and controlled. It influences the way in which a company deals with its stakeholders shareholders, customers, employees and society at large. In the wake of financial scandals such as Enron, WorldCom, and the Asian financial crisis in the late 1990s, more emphasis has been placed on making sure that there are controls in place to monitor the way businesses operate and to prevent those in authority, such as the directors, from abusing their power.

The current framework of corporate governance in Hong Kong consists of both statutory requirements and non-statutory requirements.

Hong Kong's corporate governance standards are based on principles, as is also largely the case in other countries such as the UK and Canada. Directors have more discretion than under the rule-based approach adopted in the US which specifies expected behaviour (Sarbanes-Oxley Act).

Statutory requirements cover matters such as fundamental shareholder rights, set out in the Companies Ordinance; and the disclosure of interests and insider trading (an illegal offence) in the Securities and Futures Ordinance.
