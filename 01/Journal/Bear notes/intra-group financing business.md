# intra-group financing business
'Intra-group financing business' is defined in s.14C(1) to mean, in relation to a corporation, the business of the borrowing of money from and lending of money to its associated corporations.
- - - -
## Carrying on an intra-group financing business
In a typical intra-group financing arrangement, group entities remit excess cash into a centralised pool, which is managed by a designated entity, a CTC. A corporation will only be regarded as acting in the ordinary course of its intra-group financing business where it borrows money from and lends money to its associated corporations regularly as a business with a view to earning an interest margin (DIPN 52, para 9). There must be a sufficient number of intra-group borrowing and lending transactions with a number of associated corporations, involving not an insignificant amount of funds having regard to the nature and scale of the business operations of the group concerned. The IRD will generally accept that a corporation is carrying on an intra-group financing business if (DIPN 52, para 10):
(a) It has **not less than ~four~ borrowing or lending transactions each month**;
(b) ~Each~ borrowing or lending transaction **exceeds $250,000**; and
(c) Borrowing or lending transactions are with **not less than four associated corporations** in the relevant basis period.

::Failure:: to reach the above presumptive benchmark would ~not necessarily lead to~ the conclusion that the corporation is not carrying on an intra-group financing business. The IRD will make an ~assessment based on facts~, taking into account the following factors (DIPN 52, para 9):
(a) The frequency, repetitiveness and the amount of the borrowing from and lending to associated corporations of money;
(b) Whether there is borrowing from and lending to associated corporations of money at commercial rates of interest;
(c) Whether there is a degree of system and continuity of laying out and getting back of the loan of money by way of interest and repayment of principal;
(d) The regularity and frequency of payment of interest and repayment of principal;
(e) Whether a profit is earned out of the interest differential between the borrowing and lending; and
(f) Whether the interest charged on the borrowing and lending is on an arm's length basis.

The IRD reiterated in DIPN 52 that the arm's length principle must be followed when fixing the interest rates for intra-group financing transactions, and transfer pricing adjustments may be made by the IRD.

However, a corporation that only borrows and lends money in isolated transactions would not be regarded as carrying on an intra-group financing business. On the other hand, a corporation may carry on other businesses in addition to the intra-group financing business; and a corporation may qualify even though it is largely funded by equity or bank loans.
