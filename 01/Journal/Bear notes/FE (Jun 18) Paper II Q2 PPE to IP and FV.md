# FE (Jun 18) Paper II: Q2 PPE to IP and FV
Assume that you are Ms Grace Tang, the Financial Controller of the Group. Explain to Ms Eleanor Chan, the CFO of the Group:

(a) the accounting impact of the change in the use of the Central property from a self-use property to rental purposes. (4 marks)
[[Investment property]]
- - - -
(b) the accounting impact of the change in the use of the Shatin property from rental purposes to a self-use property. (3 marks)
- - - -
(c) the fair value hierarchy classification of the Central property and Shatin property stated at fair value (Level 1, 2 or 3). (3 marks)
- - - -
## Answer
(a) Since the Central property is currently used as an owner-occupied property of the Group, it is accounted for as a property, plant and equipment. In accordance with the Group’s accounting policy, it is stated at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment losses up to the date of change in use.

The Central property will then be accounted for as an investment property upon the change in use from an owner-occupied property to leasing out for rental purposes (i.e. end of owner – occupation). In accordance with the Group’s accounting policy, it is stated at valuation. In accordance with para.62 of HKAS 40 Investment property, the resulting increase in carrying value (i.e. HK$1,000,000 - difference between the carrying amount (HK$7,000,000) and the fair value (HK$8,000,000)) would be:

(i) to the extent that the increase **reverses a previous impairment loss** (HK$600,000) for that property (Central property), the increase is recognised in ~profit or loss.~ The amount recognised in profit or loss does not exceed the amount needed to restore the carrying amount to the carrying amount that would have been determined (net of depreciation) had no impairment loss been recognised; and

(ii) any ~remaining~ part of the increase (HK$400,000) is recognised in **other comprehensive income** and increases the revaluation surplus within equity.
- - - -
(b)  Since the Shatin property is currently leased out for rental purposes by the Group, it is accounted for as an investment property. In accordance with the Group’s accounting policy, it is stated at fair value.

The Shatin property will then be accounted for as an owner-occupied property (i.e. property, plant and equipment) upon the change in use from leasing out for rental purposes to an office. In accordance with the Group’s accounting policy, it is stated at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment losses. In accordance with para.60 of HKAS 40 Investment property, the deemed cost of the Shatin property for subsequent accounting under HKAS 16 Property Plant and Equipment would be HK$15,000,000 on the date of change in use.
- - - -
(c) Based on the case background, discounted cash flow method is used in estimating the fair value of the properties while the significant inputs to the valuation include estimated future rental based on management’s estimation and discount rate.

Both the Central and Shatin properties would not be classified under **Level 1** of the fair value hierarchy as the significant inputs to the valuations (including estimated future rental and discount rate) are ~not quoted prices~ in the active markets and there are ~no identical properties~ that could exist considering the nature of the real estate.

It is also less likely that the Central and Shatin properties would be classified under **Level 2** of the fair value hierarchy as the significant inputs to the valuations, in particular, **estimated future rental and discount rate** which are based on management estimation, are ~not directly or indirectly observable~.

It is more likely that the Central and Shatin Properties will fall within **Level 3** of the fair value hierarchy as the significant inputs to the valuations (including estimated future rental and discount rate) are ~unobservable inputs~ and are ~developed using the best information available~ that the market participants would use when pricing the properties.
