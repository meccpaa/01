Exchange rate is the rate at which one country's currency can be traded in exchange for another country's currency.

[[Spot rate]] is the exchange or interest rate currently offered on a particular currency or security. The spot rate is the rate of exchange in currency for **immediate** delivery.

[[Forward rate]] is an exchange rate available now for currencies to be exchanged at a **future date**.

## 8.1.1 International currency market conventions
Certain conventions are used in the international currency market when quoting one currency against another, often referred to as a ~currency pair~.

- The first currency quoted is the base currency, and 
- the second currency quoted is the variable or counter currency. 

The rates quoted are always the price of a unit of the base currency expressed in the counter currency.

So, a quotation **£/HK$11.25** means that the price of one pound is 11.25 Hong Kong dollars. Here, £ is the base currency and HK$ is the counter currency. **Major currencies**, such as the euro, sterling and US dollar, are more likely to be the **base currency** in a currency pair.

In currency markets three letter codes are used to describe each currency which have been standardised by the International Organisation for Standardisation known as **ISO codes**. For example the above quote using ISO codes would be GBP/HKD. ISO codes for other heavily traded currencies include USD for the US dollar, EUR for the Euro and, rather less obviously, CHF for the Swiss Franc.

Additionally a **quote for a currency pair will consist of two rates** rather than one. For example:

GBP/HKD 11.2475 -11.2595

- The first rate is the price at which a ~bank will buy~ the base currency, (in this case £), and is known as the **‘bid’ price**. 
- The second rate is the price at which a ~bank will sell~ the base currency and is known as the **‘offer’ or ‘ask’ price**.
