# FE Dec 19 Paper II Section B - 5(a) severance payment - profits tax
**(a)  The profits tax implication for CHL of the severance payments made to the dismissed employees and how the severance payments should be reported in the relevant Employer’s Returns.**
**(8 marks)**
- - - -
## Answer
S.16 states that outgoings and expenses are deductible if they are incurred in the production of assessable profits for any period, and not necessarily the basis period in which such outgoings and expenses are incurred. 

S.17(1)(b) only excludes expenses not being money expended for the purpose of producing such profits. 

When these two provisions of the IRO are taken together, it is clear that an expense which is paid in a subsequent year in respect of profits chargeable to tax in a previous year are capable of deduction. 

As regards the portion of severance payment made in accordance to the EO, it has been established in *CIR v Cosmotron Manufacturing Company Limited (1997) 4 HKTC 562*that such payments were not expenses incurred to close down and terminate the taxpayer’s business. They were payments made to satisfy statutory obligation incurred in the course of running its business prior to its closure. The payments accrued as a cost of employing staff and the fact that the liability only crystalised on cessation of business did not render the payments non-deductible. 

For the above reasons, the portion of severance payment made under the EO would be tax deductible to CHL. 

As for the excess portion, there are grounds to argue that it was paid for the purpose of the preserving the reputation of CHL, and the Group as a good employer and ensured orderly conduct of its business prior to the date of closure of the Fo Tan Premise and Shatin store. Therefore, it was a normal business expenditure which would also be tax deductible to CHL (*CIR v Swire Pacific Ltd (1979) 1 HKTC 1145).*

CHL should keep sufficient documentation of its intention to substantiate the deductibility of the excess portion of the severance payments. 
According to the notes and instructions of the relevant Employer’s Returns, severance payment made in accordance with the EO is not required to be reported. 

Employers are only required to report the excess portion of the severance payment over the EO. 

CHL is recommended to add a remark on the Employer’s Returns, stating its intention of the excess portion. This will assist the dismissed employees in substantiating that the excess portion of the severance payment over the EO was not a reward for past services; therefore, it should not be regarded as assessable income.

## Panel
Question 5(a) – 8 marks 
This question concerned the tax deductibility of severance payment and the related Employer’s reporting obligations. 
The candidates’ performance was fair. Many candidates were able to analyse the deductibility of the severance payment by segregating the portion paid in accordance to Employment Ordinance (“EO”) and the portion in excess of the EO. 
Many candidates correctly cite the Cosmotron case to support that the severance payments made in accordance to the EO is tax deductible. 
However, for the potion in excess of the EO, quite a lot of candidates explained that it is non-deductible on the authority of Cosmotron case. It appears that they were not aware that the Cosmotron case only concerned severance payments paid under EO and made no comments on sums not made according to EO. 
Not many candidates were able to mount the argument that the excess portion could still be tax deductible on the basis that it was paid for the purpose of preserving the reputation of the Group as a good employer and ensured orderly conduct of its business prior to the closure of the Fo Tan Premise and Shatin store (*CIR v Swire Pacific Ltd*). 
With respect to how the severance payment should be reported in the relevant Employer’s Return, many candidates simply answered that CHL need to report the payment in the Employer’s Return IR56F without addressing how it should be reported. In addition, not many candidates were able to point that employers are only required to report the excess portion of the severance payment over the EO.
