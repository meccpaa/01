# Interpretations the provisions in the Ordinances - tax

1. **literal interpretation**: in general, unless the words taken in their natural sense could lead to an absurdity or an inconsistency with other provisions in the ordinance
2. **specific provision prevails**: whenever a specific provision conflicts with a general provision 
3. **True intent**: According to `s.19 of the Interpretation and General Clauses Ordinance (IGCO)`, where the meaning of words in a statute is unclear, an ordinance is deemed to be remedial and will receive such fair, large and liberal construction and interpretation as will best ensure the attainment of the object of the ordinance according to its true intent, meaning and spirit.
4. **DIPNs guidance**: to taxpayers and tax practitioners. The DIPNs are not binding on either the tax authority or the taxpayers
5. **Appeal procedures**: When a taxpayer and the IRD cannot agree on the interpretation of a provision in an ordinance, the taxpayer or the IRD can use the appeal procedures laid down in the IRO and seek a ruling on a question of law from the courts. The courts have, in the past, clarified the interpretation of relevant provisions in ordinances
6. **Judicial precedent**: the decision of a higher court binds all lower courts. Thus, the decisions of the Court of Final Appeal and, prior to 1 July 1997, the Privy Council of the UK, are final on all parties.

### Steps to interpreting a tax provision

As a general guide to interpreting a tax provision, we should:
1. Determine which words are defined in the ordinance. For example, most of the definitions in the IRO can be found in s.2 or other sections.
2. Give the other words their usual literal or dictionary meanings.
3. Check to see whether the IRD has given its interpretation on the subject in the DIPNs.
4. Check the case law to see if the courts have already been asked to rule on the subject.
5. Look into the background of the enactment of the relevant provision to find out the legislative intention.

To correctly interpret the wording of an ordinance, special attention must be paid to words or expressions that occur frequently and have specific meanings.
