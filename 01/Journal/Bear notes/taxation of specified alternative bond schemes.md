# taxation of specified alternative bond schemes
Because the payment and receipt of interest is prohibited under the Islamic law (i.e. Shariah), alternative financial arrangements such as 'sukuk' are developed to generate the same economic effects as conventional bonds but in a Shariah-compliant manner. Although the sukuk arrangement is economically equivalent to a debt arrangement, the prohibition of interest payments and multiple transfers of assets would lead to additional Hong Kong tax exposures for the parties involved as compared to those of a conventional debt arrangement, thus resulting in an impediment to developing the sukuk market in Hong Kong.

The Inland Revenue and Stamp Duty Legislation (Alternative Bond Schemes) (Amendment) Ordinance 2013 was enacted on 19 July 2013 to

(a) Amend the IRO to bring particular types of arrangement (some common types of Islamic financial arrangements, i.e. sukuk) that are economically equivalent to debt instruments under tax rules, for profits tax and property tax purposes, comparable to those applying to debt arrangements; and

(b) Amend the SDO to give stamp duty relief in relation to those types of arrangements. (This is discussed in chapter 8, section 8.5 on 'Relief for alternative bond schemes under ss.47E, 47F and 47G'.)

The legislation adopts a religion-neutral approach and the term 'alternative bond scheme' ('ABS') is used to denote the specified arrangements to which the tax treatment applies. Details of the specified ABS and its tax treatment are set out in Schedule 17A of the IRO ('the Schedule') (s.40AB).

### 5.6.1 Key features of alternative bond schemes

A typical ABS involves:

(a) Three parties, an originator ('O'), a bond-issuer ('BI') and bond-holders ('BHs'); and

(b) Two arrangements, a bond arrangement ('BA') and an investment arrangement ('IA').

A bond arrangement is an arrangement between a BI and BHs under which:

(a) The O, the party in need of funding, sets up a special purpose vehicle ('SPV') as the BI;

(b) The BI issues alternative bonds ('ABs') to the BHs in return for the bond proceeds that the BHs pay for purchasing the ABs; and

(c) The BI enters into an IA with the O and undertakes to use the investment return and disposal proceeds under the IA to fund the payment (the redemption payment) for redeeming the ABs and other payments (additional payments) to the BHs.

The redemption payment is economically equivalent to the principal repayment in conventional bonds. Additional payments are periodic distributions to the BHs out of the income or gains derived from the IA and are functionally similar to interest payments in conventional bonds.

Similar to conventional bonds, ABs may be issued at a discount or redeemed at a premium. The par value above the discount price and the redemption premium would form part of the redemption payment and not additional payments.

The ABs represent the rights and interests of the BHs in or in relation to the specified asset which was acquired under an IA by the BI with the bond proceeds. This is the most significant feature distinguishing ABs from conventional bonds which only represent the debt owed by the BI to the BHs.

An investment arrangement is an arrangement between a BI and an O under which the BI:

(a) Uses the bond proceeds to carry out some specified asset transactions or activities with a view to generating an investment return during the ABS term; and

(b) Uses the investment return, together with the disposal proceeds of the specified asset by the end of the ABS term to fund the redemption payment and additional payments to the BHs.

### Example 20 (Adapted from DIPN 50, Example 1)

Co-BI issued ABs to Co-BH under a BA. Co-BI entered into an IA with Co-O under which the bond proceeds were used to acquire an asset to generate income and gains to fund payments to Co-BH.

The tripartite structure comprised a BA and an IA with Co-O, Co-BI and Co-BH as the participants. The structure fell within the definition of ABS and the provisions in the Schedule would apply to the structure if other conditions are satisfied.

### 5.6.2 Arrangements covered by the legislation

An ABS is a scheme consisting of a BA and an IA that meet the description in s.2 of the Schedule. An ABS is a specified ABS if the IA in the scheme meets the descriptions in ss.5 to 12 of the Schedule (s.4 of the Schedule).

To qualify for the special tax treatment, the IA has to be one of the following four 'specified IAs' in s.5 of the Schedule, and the terms of the ABS and the IA remain the same throughout the specified term:

(1) A lease arrangement ('Ijara') under which a BI enters into a lease in respect of an acquired asset with an O to generate an investment return (s.6 of the Schedule).

(2) A profits sharing arrangement ('Musharaka' and 'Mudaraba') under which a BI enters into a business undertaking ('BU') with an O to carry on business activities to generate an investment return (s.7 of the Schedule).

(3) A purchase and sale arrangement ('Murabaha') under which a BI sells an acquired asset to an O with a markup to generate an investment return (s.8 of the Schedule).

(4) An agency arrangement ('Wakala') under which a BI appoints an O as its agent to manage an acquired asset to generate an investment return (s.9 of the Schedule).

### Example 21 (Adapted from DIPN 50, Example 3)

Co-BI issued ABs to Co-BH under a BA. The IA between Co-BI and Co-O was a lease arrangement. The terms of the ABS and IA remained unchanged throughout the specified term.

Since the IA was a specified IA and the terms of the ABS and IA remained unchanged throughout the specified term, the ABS was a specified ABS. If the lease arrangement had been changed to an agency arrangement before the ABs were fully redeemed or cancelled, the ABS would not have been a specified ABS.

### 5.6.3 Qualifying conditions for special tax treatment

In order for a specified ABS to qualify for the special tax treatment, the BA and the IA must be a qualified BA ('QBA') and a qualified IA ('QIA'), meet the specified qualifying conditions spelt out in ss.13 to 20 of the Schedule and regarded as a debt arrangement.

Conditions to qualify as a qualified bond arrangement

Subject to some disqualifying events (see section 5.6.5), the BA in a specified ABS is a QBA at any time (the material time) if the scheme at the material time complies with, and from the commencement of the specified term of the scheme up to the material time has always complied with, the following five conditions (ss.13(1), 13(3), 13(5) and 28 of the Schedule):

(1) Reasonable commercial return condition

Both (i) the maximum total amount of the bond return that may be payable to the BHs under the terms of the specified ABS and (ii) the total amount actually paid to the BHs must not exceed an amount that would be a reasonable commercial return on money borrowed of the amount of the bond proceeds (s.14 of the Schedule). This is to ensure that the return from the AB is economically equivalent to the return from a financial liability.

### Example 23 (Adapted from DIPN 50, Example 19)

An AB of par value $100,000 with a two-year tenor was issued at $98,000 and was redeemed at par. The holder received 4 half-yearly payments of $1,000 each.

The bond return to the holder at the end of the specified term

= $100,000 – $98,000 + $1,000 x 4 = $6,000

If the bonds were redeemed in two equal instalments, the bond return to the holder at the end of first year

= $50,000 – ($98,000 x $50,000/$100,000) + $1,000 x 2

= $3,000

(2) Bond arrangement as financial liability condition

The BA in a specified ABS must be treated as a financial liability of the BI in accordance with either the Hong Kong Financial Reporting Standards ('FRSs') or International FRSs, or would be so treated if the BI applied those standards (s.15 of the Schedule).

(3) Hong Kong connection condition

The ABs must be
(i) Listed on the Hong Kong stock exchange, or
(ii) Issued in good faith and in the course of carrying on business in Hong Kong, or
(iii) Marketed in Hong Kong, or

(iv) Lodged with and cleared by the Central Moneymarkets Unit operated by the Monetary Authority (s.16 of the Schedule).

This is to ensure that the ABs will have the requisite degree of nexus with Hong Kong in a way that it would help promote the Hong Kong Sukuk platform.

(4) Maximum term length condition

The term of a specified ABS must not exceed 15 years. The Financial Secretary may, by notice published in the Gazette, amend this period (s.17 of the Schedule).

(5) Arrangements performed according to terms condition

The specified ABS must be performed according to the prescribed features and characteristics (s.18 of the Schedule) so as to prevent any abuse by delaying the disposal of the specified asset to an infinite period.

A specified ABS will not cease to comply because of a delay of not more than 30 days in disposing of the specified asset.

The Commissioner may disregard a delay in disposing of the specified asset as a breach of this condition if prescribed conditions are otherwise met, and the Commissioner is satisfied that there was a reasonable excuse for the delay (s.28 of the Schedule).

Example 24 (Adapted from DIPN 50, Example 20)

Co-BI issued ABs with a specified term of 12 years. As a result of liquidity problem, Co-O unilaterally delayed the purchase of the specified asset from Co-BI at the end of the specified term for 28 days.

The condition would be treated as satisfied under s.18(2) of the Schedule. If the purchase was delayed for three months, s.28 of the Schedule would allow the Commissioner to treat the condition as having been satisfied. If the tenor of the alternative bonds had been 15 years, the 'maximum term length condition' would not have been complied with.

Conditions to qualify as a qualified investment arrangement

Subject to some disqualifying events, the specified IA in a specified ABS is a QIA at any time (the material time) if (ss.13(2), 13(4), 13(5) and 28 of the Schedule):

(1) The BA in the scheme at the material time is, and from the commencement of the specified term of the scheme up to the material time has always been, a QBA; and

(2) The scheme at the material time complies with, and from the commencement of the specified term of the scheme up to the material time has always complied with, the following two conditions:

(i)

Bond-issuer as conduit condition

Under the terms of a specified ABS, the maximum total amount of the investment return that may be receivable by the BI must not exceed the maximum total amount of the bond return payable to the BHs.

Further, the total amount of the investment return actually received by the BI must not exceed the total amount of the bond return actually paid to the BHs (s.19 of the Schedule).

To determine whether the condition is met, the investment return paid or payable under the specified IA in a period will be compared with the bond return paid or payable under the BA in the specified ABS in that period. If the investment return paid or payable exceeds the bond return paid or payable, the condition is not met and the specified IA is not a QIA since it is not economically equivalent to a debt arrangement (DIPN 50, para 100).

(ii) Investment arrangement as financial liability condition

The IA in a specified ABS must be treated as a financial liability of the O in accordance with either the Hong Kong FRSs or International FRSs, or would be so treated if the O applied those standards (s.20 of the Schedule).

5.6.4 Special profits tax treatments for specified alternative bond scheme

To ensure that ABSs are accorded equivalent profits tax treatments to a conventional BA, the BA and the IA are treated as a 'debt arrangement' when the qualifying conditions of a specified ABS are met. Effective from 19 July 2013, ss.21 and 22 of the Schedule amend the relevant provisions of the IRO, and the amendments are summarised as follows:
![](taxation%20of%20specified%20alternative%20bond%20schemes/5AB1C4F4-57B6-4392-84E9-35D5E7217F76.png)
Tax treatment for qualified bond arrangements under s.21 of Schedule 17A

A QBA is treated as a debt arrangement such that:

(a) The bond proceeds are regarded as money borrowed by the BI from the BHs;

(b) The additional payments are regarded as interest payable on money borrowed by the BI from the BHs;

(c) The BI is not regarded as a trustee in respect of the specified asset;

(d) The BHs are not regarded as having any legal or beneficial interest in the specified asset;

(e) If the rights in the ABs are transferable by delivery, the concessionary tax rate under s.14A or the exemption under s.26A will apply provided that the condition stipulated for the concession and exemption are satisfied;

(f) If the rights in the ABs are transferable by delivery, the ABs are regarded as certificates of deposit for the purposes of ss.15(1)(j), (k), (l) and (la), and the making of redemption payment for the ABs is regarded as the redemption on maturity or presentment of certificates of deposit;

(g) The additional payments are accorded the same tax treatment under s.16(2)(f) as interest payable on conventional bonds;

(h) The ABs are regarded as 'bonds' within the definition of 'securities' for the purposes of s.20AC and item 1 of Schedule 16; and

(i) The QBA is not regarded as a mutual fund, unit trust or similar investment scheme for the purposes of s.26A.

Tax treatment for qualified investment arrangements under s.22 of Schedule 17A

A QIA is regarded as a debt arrangement such that:

(a) The acquisition cost of the specified assets, which is funded by the bond proceeds from the BA, is regarded as money borrowed by the O from the BI;

(b) The Investment return payable is regarded as interest on the money borrowed by the O from the BI;

(c) The BI is not regarded as having any legal or beneficial interest in the specified asset;

(d) Any specified asset transaction between the O and the BI is disregarded;

(e) The specified asset, or part of it, acquired/ disposed of by the BI from/to a third party is regarded as acquired/ disposed of by the O directly from/to the third party;

(f) The income, expenditure (including depreciation allowances), profits, gains or losses arising from the specified asset are regarded as those of the O; and

(g) The ABs are regarded as debentures or instruments and the BI is treated as an associated corporation of the O for the purpose of s.16(2)(f)(iii).

Furthermore, where the QIA involves an interest in a BU as the specified asset,

(a) The BU and any acquisition or disposal of an interest in the BU are disregarded;

(b) Any asset transaction between the O and the BU is disregarded;

(c) Any asset acquired/ disposed of by the BU from/to a third party is regarded as acquired/ disposed of directly by the O from/to that third party;

(d) Any other business activities carried on by the BU during the specified term are regarded as business activities carried on by the O directly; and

(e) The income, expenditure (including depreciation allowances), profits, gains or losses arising from an asset held by, or other business activities carried on by the BU are regarded as those of the O.

### 5.6.5 Disqualification and withdrawal of special tax treatment
Key terms in s.13(5) of Schedule 17A
'Bond arrangement disqualifying event' means:
(a) The ABS is not a specified ABS at any time during the specified term of the scheme; or
(b) The specified ABS fails to comply with any of the five qualifying conditions for a BA at any time during the specified term.

'Investment arrangement disqualifying event' means:
(a) The ABS is not a specified ABS at any time during the specified term of the scheme; or
(b) The specified ABS fails to comply with any of the five qualifying conditions for a BA at any time during the specified term (i.e. the BA in the specified ABS is not a QBA); or
(c) The specified ABS fails to comply with any of the two qualifying conditions for a specified IA at any time during the specified term.

In the event that a BA in a specified ABS is disqualified, the IA in the specified ABS is also disqualified, but not vice versa.
The occurrence of a BA/IA disqualifying event at any time during the specified term will result in a QBA or QIA being regarded as never having been qualified (ss.13(3) and (4) of the Schedule), leading to a withdrawal of the special tax treatment under ss.21 or 22 of the Schedule retrospectively from the commencement of the specified term. The BA or the IA in the specified ABS will remain an unqualified BA or IA even if the scheme complies with the specified conditions later and throughout the rest of the specified term.

The tax liabilities of the BI or the O for all relevant years of assessment have to be re-computed and assessments, additional assessments or reassessments and refund of any tax paid in excess ('specified assessment') under ss.60 or 79 are to be issued (s.27(2) of the Schedule). The time periods specified in s.60 are to run after the expiry of the year in which the earliest BA or IA disqualifying event has occurred if that year falls after the relevant year of assessment. (s.27(3) of the Schedule).

The provisions in the IRO relating to a notice of assessment, appeal and other proceedings apply to specified assessments and any tax charged under the modified ss.60 or 79. However, s.27(8) of the Schedule provides that where the BI or the O objects to a specified assessment for any year of assessment disputing the disqualification of the BA or IA, the objection is treated as objections to all specified assessments made for all years of assessment because of that disqualification.

The Commissioner is empowered to disregard a delay of more than 30 days in disposing of the specified asset where there is a reasonable excuse in relation to the 'arrangements performed according to terms' condition (s.28(2) of the Schedule).

A claimant is required to notify the Commissioner within 30 days after the occurrence of a BA/IA disqualifying event. A failure to do so may constitute an offence and result in penalties under ss.80 and 82A (ss.26(1) and (2) of the Schedule).

However, notification will not be required if:

(a) The assessment or assessments for any year of assessment, made on the basis that the claim that the BA or IA is a QBA or QIA is not accepted, have become final and conclusive under s.70;

(b) The specified assessment or assessments for any year of assessment, made on the basis that the BA or IA is disqualified because of an earlier disqualifying event, have become final and conclusive under s.70 (DIPN 50, para 115).

#### Example 26 (Adapted from DIPN 50, Example 26)

In a specified ABS, 99% of the ABs issued were subscribed by one BH. The IA of the ABS was a profits sharing arrangement. The ABS complied with the seven qualifying conditions. Therefore, its BA and IA had been regarded as a QBA and a QIA respectively. At the end of the specified term, the BI, instead of waiving the excessive profits in favour of the O according to the terms of the scheme, distributed all excessive profits to the BHs.

Two BA disqualifying events occurred. The scheme failed to comply with the 'reasonable commercial return condition' and the 'arrangements performed according to terms condition'. Both the BA and the IA are to be regarded as never having been a QBA and never having been a QIA respectively. The special tax treatment accorded to them would be revoked.

### 5.6.6 Record keeping and limitation periods

The tax regime for ABS allows ABs to have a specified term up to 15 years. Revocation of the special tax treatment is retrospective from the commencement of the specified term. To align with the specified term, s.25 of the Schedule modifies s.51C and requires the BI and the O to retain records in respect of the specified ABS at least until the expiry of seven years after the completion of the transactions, acts or operations to which they relate; or the expiry of three years after the end of the specified term of the specified ABS, whichever is the later. Failure to do so will be treated as a failure to comply with s.51C and will entail penalties under s.80.

However, the modified record-keeping period ceases to apply if:

(a) The assessment or assessments for any year of assessment, made on the basis that the claim that the BA or IA is a QBA or QIA is not accepted, have become final and conclusive under s.70;

(b) The specified assessment or assessments for any year of assessment, made on the basis that the BA or IA is disqualified, have become final and conclusive under s.70.

To align with the specified term which is up to 15 years, s.27(3) of the Schedule modifies s.60 by allowing the limitation periods to run after the expiry of the year of disqualification of a BA/IA if that year falls after the year of assessment. Similarly, s.79 is modified by s.27(5) of the Schedule to extend the time period for refund in case of disqualification. The period of six years is to run after the expiry of the year of disqualification if that year falls after the year of assessment.

5.6.7 Advance ruling

Where a person intends to enter into a specified ABS as the O and claims that the specified IA is a QIA, the person may make an application for an advance ruling. Advance ruling is discussed in chapter 9, section 7 on 'Advance ruling system'.
