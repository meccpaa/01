# FE (Jun 18) Paper I: Section A Q1(b) substantive tests - revenue
[[FE (Jun 18) Paper I: case]]

(b) Identify the different revenue streams of Grand Music Group and suggest one substantive audit procedure that can be performed to address the accuracy assertion of each revenue stream. Justify whether the different revenue streams can be tested on an aggregated basis for Grand Music Group’s audit purposes. (9 marks)

## Answer
Revenue streams of Grand Music Group
(i) Rental income
Substantive procedures: perform **reasonableness test** based on contracts or perform **test of details** to ~trace to the underlying information~ such as invoices / contracts, customer’s acknowledgements and deposits received.

(ii) Consignment sales of Best Performance’s musical instruments
Substantive procedures: perform **test of details** to ~trace~ to the invoices / contracts, delivery notes, customer’s acknowledgements, cash received, etc, as well as to calculate the ~consignment sales revenue~ that should be recognised by the Group.

(iii) Normal sales of musical instruments
Substantive procedures: perform **test of details** to ~trace to the invoices and cash receipts~.

(iv) Income from different courses
Substantive procedures: since the ~types of course~ vary with the impact on discounts, coupons etc, perform **test of details** to ~trace~ to the **underlying data** to assess whether the ~amount recognised is accurate~.
[[revenue - audit procedures]]

Whether or not the above revenue streams can be **tested on an aggregate** basis depends on whether they are ~subject to the same risk of material misstatements~, whether they are ~processed and recorded in the same way~ and whether the ~controls~ applied are the same.

Since the **nature** of the revenue is different, processed through different **systems** and different **controls** are applied to different revenue streams, they ::should not be tested on an aggregated basis:: for Grand Music Group’s reporting purposes.
