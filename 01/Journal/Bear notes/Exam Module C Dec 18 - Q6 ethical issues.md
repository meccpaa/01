# Exam Module C: Dec 18 - Q6 ethical issues

(b) XYZ & Co is the auditor of Company Y. Company Y has invited XYZ & Co to participate in an ~advisory project proposal~ relating to operational effectiveness and efficiency review. The audit team would like to give Susana an ~expensive festive hamper~ while the advisory project is under tendering. ## Solutions
Answer 6(a)

Susana is the Chief Financial Officer and Executive Director of Company Y, who has **self-interest** in Company Y’s financial results.

A **listed company** usually has ~internal policy and procedures~ regarding acquisition of the company’s interest by directors and key management. Before Susana purchases the shares of Company Y in the market, she should **check** with the internal policy and procedures to ensure that she properly follows those. She should also ~disclose her plan of purchase~ to those charged with governance (i.e. audit committee).

Since Susana’s plan of purchasing shares of Company Y in the market may pose concern about ::using confidential information for personal advantage::, Susana should also ensure that she has **complied with all the laws and regulations**, such as sections 270 and 291 of the Securities and Futures Ordinance regarding insider dealing.
[[Insider dealing]]
Answer 6(b)

Since the audit team and the advisory team are two separate teams with different reporting lines within XYZ & Co, any [[self-review threat - old]] is minimised.

The audit team ::should not give the festive hamper:: to Susana during the advisory project tender period. The tender period is sensitive. It may pose [[self-interest threat - old]] to XYZ & Co and weaken XYZ & Co’s [[independence in appearance]] on bidding the advisory project during this sensitive period. In addition, an expensive festive hamper is definitely not trivial and inconsequential, which may be seen to be intended to influence the judgement of Company Y.

If the advisory project is accepted, the total non-audit fees will be twice the total audit fees for the year. The higher total non-audit fees comparing to the total audit fee is a [[self-interest threat - old]]. XYZ & Co should discuss with the audit committee the **nature** of the non-audit service and consult the audit committee’s view on whether the non-audit service may pose concern on the **independence** of XYZ & Co as Company Y’s auditor before XYZ & Co commences the advisory project. XYZ & Co should summarise and report the total audit fees and non-audit fees to the audit committee and such information should be disclosed in the annual report of Company Y.

Answer 6(c)
[[specific guidance: confidentiality]][[Confidentiality]]
As a professional accountant, Peter has a **duty of confidentiality** to his client. HKICPA’s Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants states that a professional accountant who acquires sensitive information in the course of his work, should not use, nor appear to use, that information to his own advantage or to the advantage of any third party with which he is connected.

Though Peter did not disclose the details of the spin off project to his friend intentionally and he did not disclose the spin off project relating to Company Y, Peter **violated** his duty of confidentiality to Company Y to keep Company Y’s information confidential.

In addition, Peter frequently shares his work location on his **social media** network. This may tip off his friend that the spin off project is related to Company Y. If Peter’s friend took advantage of this information, Peter would also be deemed to violate the relevant laws and regulations on misusing insider information and be liable to the loss to Company Y.

Peter should be **more sensitive and act with due diligence** in the future on confidential information of his clients and stop mentioning this information to anyone, including his close family members. Peter should also stop sharing his work location on his social media account.
