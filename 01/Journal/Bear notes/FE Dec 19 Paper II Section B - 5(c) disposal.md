# FE Dec 19 Paper II Section B - 5(c) disposal
**(c)  Whether the profit derived from the disposal of the Fo Tan Premise is subject to profits tax.**
**(5 marks)**
- - - -
## Answer
S.14 of the IRO specifically excludes profits arising from the sale of capital assets. Whether the disposal of an asset would be regarded as a realisation of a capital asset or a business of trading in property depends upon the facts and circumstances of each case. 
In this regard, the report of the Royal Commission on the Taxation of Profits and Income in 1955 identified the following six “badges of trade”: 

* subject matter of realisation; 
* length of ownership; 
* frequency of similar transactions; 
* supplementary work done on the asset realised; 
* circumstances leading to disposal; and 
* motive/intention.

The original intention or motive of acquiring the asset is the most crucial consideration (*Simmons v IRC 53 TC 461 and All Best Wishes Limited v CIR (1992) 3 HKTC 750*). The fact that the Fo Tan Premise (“the Property”) has been used by CHL for its furniture trading business since acquisition is a strong indication that the primary intention of acquiring the Property was for self-use. 
The long period of ownership of over 18 years further confirms that the Property was CHL’s capital asset. 
The disposal was driven by CHL contemplating business restructuring. Sale proceeds from the disposal of the Property will be used to fund the business restructuring. 
As CHL did not perform any enhancement work on the Property to make it more saleable, nor conduct any extensive marketing activities to promote or solicit buyers for the Property (it was an unsolicited offer from PIHKL), the Property would be regarded as a capital asset of CHL. As such, any profit arising from the disposal of the Property would not be subject to profits tax. 
Whether or not CHL has undertaken any buy-sell of immovable properties in the past should have minimal impact on the above conclusion.

## Panel
This question asked candidates to evaluate whether the disposal gain in respect of the Fo Tan Premise that has been used by the Company for over 18 years would be regarded as capital in nature and non-taxable. 
The candidates’ performance was good. Most of them were able to apply the “six badges of trade” test to the given facts to evaluate the taxability of the disposal gain. 
However, a few candidates did not read the question carefully and discussed the balancing adjustments that need to be made upon disposal of the Fo Tan Premise, which was not required.
