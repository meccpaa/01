# FE Dec 19 Paper II Section A - 1(a) Ethics - audit fee
(a) In relation to the Thomas’ proposal for the revised audit fee arrangement, analyse whether Peter and the audit partner should accept Thomas’ proposal with reference to the HKICPA Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants.
(6 marks)
- - - -
## Answer
Peter and the audit partner should not accept the proposal by Thomas. 
Peter should assess whether the 30% reduction in audit fee would be considered as a significant low fee that creates threat to auditor’s objectivity. The fee strategy may cause a self-interest threat and call into question the professional competence of, and due care owed by, the auditor. Both independence and quality of work may be compromised, as it may be difficult to perform the engagement in accordance with applicable technical and professional standards for the fee charged. 

Thomas offers that the 30% reduction in audit fee would be compensated from wooden furniture worth of HK$800,000 at cost (where the fair value of these furniture would be significantly higher than HK$600,000, being the reduction in audit fee); this might be considered a gift from the audit client. Unless the value of the gift is trivial and inconsequential, a member of an engagement team should decline any offers which may be seen to be intended to influence the judgement of a professional accountant. 

In addition, if Peter and the audit partner, without the permission of YL & Co, accept the proposal for receiving HK$800,000 worth of wooden furniture as a compensation to the audit fee, then they might be deemed as accepting an advantage as a reward or inducement for doing any act or showing favour in relation to YL & Co’s business, and would thus commit an offence under the Prevention of Bribery Ordinance. 

For the additional audit fee, depending on the audited results of the Group for the current year, it is considered as a contingent fee arrangement which is prohibited under the Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants for audit client, as payment arrangements based on outcomes create self-interest and advocacy threats which cannot be reduced to acceptable levels through the application of suitable safeguards.

## Panel
The question required candidates to analyse whether the audit manager and the audit partner should accept client’s proposal of audit fee arrangement, which included a significant reduction in amount, gift of client’s inventory and a contingent audit fee, with reference to the HKICPA Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants. 
The candidates’ performance was fair. Candidates were able to demonstrate their understanding of the question and analysed the three portions of the client’s audit fee proposal referring to the Code of Ethics. However, some candidates did not read the question carefully and analysed the acceptability of the client’s proposal in respect of each of the three elements of the proposal, instead of providing an overall conclusion as to whether the audit manager and the audit partner should accept client’s proposal considering all the elements of the proposal.
