# Holiday journey benefits
[[Holiday journey benefits prior to 1 April 2003]], 

Under s.9(2A)(c), any amount paid by an employer in connection with a holiday journey for the benefits of the employee will be assessable to salaries tax.

The IRD issued DIPN No. 41 in August 2003 to provide guidance on the taxation of holiday journey benefits and the basis of assessment. According to DIPN No. 41, 

- all payments made by an employer will be subject to salaries tax regardless of whether the benefit is convertible into cash or whether it is the employee's own liability for the benefit and the assessable amount is based on the actual amount paid by the employer.
- In case a trip is taken **partly for business** and partly for holiday purposes, the benefit will be exempt from salaries tax if it can be established that the holiday is ~merely incidental to a business trip~. However, in the case that an ::identifiable part of the journey is taken for holiday purposes::, the expenses relating to that part of the journey will be chargeable to salaries tax.
- If the expenses are **not distinct and separable**, they may have to be ~apportioned~, generally on a '**holiday-days basis**', to ascertain the amount attributable to the part of the journey taken for holiday purposes. The cost of travel to the destination would usually not be apportioned as that cost would be incurred regardless of the holiday element.
- Where a business trip **spans a weekend**, the weekend does not necessarily comprise a holiday. However, where a weekend comes at the ~start or end~ of the business trip, such days would be regarded as **a holiday**, with costs apportioned to determine a taxable benefit.
- Where an employee is required to **travel to more than one location** in a single business trip, and whether due to routing or other reasons, stopovers are made, the **stopovers** will be regarded as ~incidental~ to the business journey if they are considered ~reasonable~.
- Where an employer **organises a holiday journey** for its employees on a group basis and ~costs are not distinct and separable~, **apportionment** should be made on a head count basis.
- Home leave will be specifically caught as a taxable benefit whether the employee travels home or elsewhere.
- Where it can be established that a trip is **not for holiday purposes**, such as ~relocation~ of an employee and his/her family at the beginning or end of an assignment, the relevant costs borne would fall ::outside the scope:: of the tax legislation and no taxable benefit would arise. As a concession, any visits to other destinations en route to or from Hong Kong would be disregarded.
