# Intra-group transfers of non-current assets
It is common for group companies to transfer non-current assets from one to another depending on where they are needed. Often the transferor makes a profit on such a transaction. As above, this profit is unrealised until such time as the non-current asset is sold outside the group and therefore it must be eliminated on consolidation.

Here there is also the extra complication of depreciation, which must, for the purpose of the consolidated accounts, be based on the original cost of the asset to the group.

There are two possible approaches to consolidation adjustments in respect of intra-group transfers of non-current assets:

1. Adjustments are made to:
- The selling company's accounts in respect of profit or loss on disposal, and 
- The buying company's accounts in respect of the reversal of excess depreciation.

2. Adjustments are made to the selling company's accounts in respect of both profit or loss on disposal and the reversal of excess depreciation.

The first approach may seem more obvious, and this is the approach that is used in this LP and should be used in HKICPA exams.

The second approach appears less logical, but can be explained as follows: as each year of an asset's useful life post-transfer passes, a proportion of the initial unrealised profit (or loss) eliminated in the selling company's accounts becomes realised, and therefore is recognised by it.
