# FE (Jun 17) Paper I: Q1(a)(i) common consideration - planning inventory observation
Common items that have to be considered for both approaches:

- **Specific risks** of material misstatement identified for the inventory based on the nature of the inventory items – any slow-moving inventory with a net realisable value problem, proper storage to maintain the quality and avoid obsolescence, etc.

- Coverage of **locations** – West Wine’s warehouses, retail shops, goods-in-transits (from suppliers, delivering to the distributors), etc.

- **Controls** executed by the Group over the **movement of the inventory** – how does the new in-house developed inventory system record the inventory movement?

- **Controls** executed by the Group to **distinguish the ownership of the inventory** – the Group’s inventory, consignment stocks from suppliers, samples from suppliers, customers’ stocks saved in the warehouses, etc.

- Identification of **high value items** – proportion and location of the expensive wine

- Consideration of the need from an **expert** – do we have the knowledge to verify the value of the high-value expensive wine which looks similar to the others?

- **Reconciliation** of inventory record – what are the details recorded on the standard count sheet and how are they reconciled to the inventory record?

- **Adjustment** made to the inventory record – How does the management investigate the counting differences before they approve the adjustments?

- Inventory count instructions – are the instructions clear enough with details on how the inventory count should be performed?
