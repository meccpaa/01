# Corporate Treasury Centre
The Inland Revenue (Amendment) (No. 2) Ordinance 2016 was gazetted on 3 June 2016 which gives profits tax concession to qualifying corporate treasury centres ("CTCs") and makes provisions for profits tax purposes regarding interests on money borrowed from or lent to associated corporations.

In essence, a CTC is an "in-house bank" within a multinational group focusing on the optimal procurement and usage of capital for the operations of the entire group. Typical CTCs perform the functions of intra-group financing, optimising multi-currency cash management and liquidity management, cash pooling, central or regional processing of payments to vendors or suppliers for the corporate group, conducting transactions for financial or treasury-related risk management, and supporting the raising of capital by the group.

S.14D(1) of the IRO provides that a corporation that is a qualifying CTC for a year of assessment is entitled to have its qualifying profits for that year of assessment charged at one-half of the profits tax rate specified in Schedule 8 to the Ordinance.

DIPN 52 was issued in September 2016 setting out the IRD's interpretation and practice in relation to the relevant provisions under the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (No. 2) Ordinance 2016.
