# borrowing from commercial banks
Although the credit role of commercial banks has declined over the last two decades, they still represent the **largest single source of financing for short-term corporate needs**. For small- to medium-size companies, banks are often the primary source of short-term credit. Banks may be the alternative lender selected by larger corporations unable to borrow in the commercial paper market.

In view of the current financial problems with banks worldwide, corporate treasurers must be concerned with problems relating to the ::[[capital adequacy of banks]]::. Capital adequacy is a regulatory issue that deals with the **level of equity that a bank must have** relative to its loan and deposit base. Banks with capital adequacy problems may be ~restricted by regulatory agencies~ in the type and amounts of loans they can grant. In addition, most of the bank regulatory agencies have mandated broad increases in the quality of loans in financial institutions' portfolios, further restricting bank lending.
