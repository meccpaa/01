# application controls - IT audit
As we saw in Chapter 11, application controls are particular to an application and may have a direct impact on the processing of individual transactions and pertain to specific computer applications. They include controls that help to ensure the proper authorisation, completeness, accuracy, and validity of transactions, maintenance and other types of data input.

## Audit risk assessment procedures
### Controls over input: completeness
* Inquiring into and observing the operation of manual or programmed agreement of control totals 
* Inspecting document counts 
* Inspecting evidence of edit checks of input data 
* Inspecting output of numerical sequence checks for evidence of manual follow-up of exception reports 
* Inspecting evidence of one-for-one checking 
* Inspecting programmed matching of input to an expected input control file 
* Inspecting and observing documented procedures over resubmission of rejected controls

### Controls over input: accuracy
* Inquiring into and testing programs to check data fields on input transactions 
* Inspecting evidence of manual scrutiny of output and reconciliation to source 
* Inspecting agreement of control totals (manual/programmed)

### Controls over input: authorisation
Inspecting for documentary evidence of manual checks

### Controls over processing
* Inspecting evidence of batch reconciliations 
* Observing for existence of screen warnings can prevent people logging out before processing is complete 
* Inspecting reports on unprocessed/ uncleared transactions (for example unpaid invoices)

### Controls over master files and standing data
* Inspecting evidence of one-to-one checking
* Inspecting evidence of cyclical reviews of all master files and standing data
* Inspecting record counts (number of documents processed) and hash totals (for example, the total of all the payroll numbers) used when master files are used to ensure no deletions
* Observing controls over the deletion of accounts that have no current balance
