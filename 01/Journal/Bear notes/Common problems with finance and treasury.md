# Common problems with finance and treasury
(a) Problems within finance and treasury may contribute to business insolvency. Cash management. The treasury department may not manage cash flows adequately. Cash flow forecasts may be inaccurate, or cash flows may not be monitored as frequently as they should be. There is a risk that a company may find itself without sufficient cash to meet its payment obligations (for example, loan repayments and payments of operating costs, such as wages and salaries).

(b) Poor relationship with bank. The treasury department may fail to maintain a sufficiently close relationship with its bank. As a consequence, the bank may be reluctant to increase the company’s overdraft borrowing facility, when the company asks for this. Occasionally, a bank might even withdraw or reduce the company’s overdraft borrowing limit.

(c) Inadequate hedging of financial risk. A company may fail to hedge its exposures to financial risk, for example with derivatives, and as a consequence suffer a severe loss when foreign exchange rates or interest rates move adversely.

(d) Financial speculation. On the other hand, the treasury department may speculate in derivatives, in the hope of making a profit on their financial dealings. Excessive financial speculation may result in financial collapse, if market prices move adversely and losses are incurred on the speculative positions.

(e) Fraud within the treasury department. Occasionally, a company may suffer large losses due to unauthorised speculation by individuals in the treasury department.

(f) Excessive borrowing. A company may borrow more than it can afford, and as a consequence fail to meet its debt repayment obligations. The treasury department may give insufficient attention to loan covenants and the possible consequences of breaching any covenant.

A variety of **preventive measures** can be taken to deal with these problems.

(a) A company should make regular cash flow forecasts, and monitor actual cash flows. The frequency of cash flow forecasting will depend on the nature of the company’s cash flows.

(b) A company should maintain regular dialogue with its relationship bank, and have suitable arrangements in place for borrowing to cover short-term cash shortages.

(c) The board should approve the company’s policy on financial gearing and borrowing limits, and the finance department should be required to operate within the policy guidelines.

(d) Similarly the company should have a policy on hedging financial risks, and if the treasury department is allowed to speculate with financial derivatives there should be strict limits on permissible exposures.

(e) There should be adequate segregation of duties within the treasury department, so that the same individual is not able to make trades in derivatives, account for them and handle payments and receipts on the derivatives trades.
