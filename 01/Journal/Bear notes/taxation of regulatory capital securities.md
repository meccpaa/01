# taxation of regulatory capital securities
RCSs are financial instruments that have features of both debt and equity and may be written-down or converted into ordinary shares to offset losses at certain points of time. They are issued by FIs under the Banking (Capital) Rules or under the equivalent laws or regulatory requirements of another member jurisdiction of the Basel Committee to meet the capital adequacy requirements of Basel III by increasing bank liquidity and decreasing bank leverage. Prior to the enactment of the 2016 Amendment (No. 2) Ordinance, RCSs were not regarded as debt instruments and their distributions were not deductible for profits tax purposes. In order to remove these adverse tax consequences and to maintain a level playing field for FIs in Hong Kong, new legislation was enacted on 26 May 2016:

(a) To provide for a debt treatment of qualifying RCSs with the resultant tax deductions for distributions; and

(b) To treat RCSs not as shares, hence no stamp duty is payable on the sale and purchase of RCSs. (This is discussed in chapter 8, section 4.1.1 on 'Regulatory capital securities'.)

The IRD issued DIPN 53 in February 2017 to provide guidance on the application of the profits tax provisions in relation to RCSs.

### 5.8.1 Tax treatment of regulatory capital securities

Effective from 3 June 2016, RCSs that satisfy the prescribed conditions shall be treated as follows:

(a) RCSs are to be treated as debt securities (s.17B(1)(a);

(b) Distributions made by the issuer in respect of the RCSs (other than a repayment of the paidup amount) are to be treated as an interest expense eligible for a tax deduction under ss.16(1)(a), 16(2)(a) and 16(2AA) as provided in s.17B(1)(b);

(c) Fair value accounting is disregarded in determining the profits of the issuer and the issuer's specified connected person ('SCP') in relation to RCSs (ss.17C(2) and 17D(2));

(d) For the issuer of a RCS and the issuer's SCP, a sum representing the paid-up amount of a RCS on a conversion, write-down or subsequent write-up of the RCS arising as a result of the issuer hitting a regulatory trigger or nearing insolvency is not to be treated as a trading receipt and is not tax deductible (ss.17C(3) and (4) and ss.17D(3) and (4)); and

(e) Distributions received from RCSs and the profits from the disposal or redemption of RCSs are to be treated as taxable trading receipts under ss.15(1)(f), (g), (i), (ia), (j),(k) , (l) and (la).

Transitional provisions are set out in Schedule 36 to grant tax deductions for distributions made after 3 June 2016 in respect of RCSs issued prior to 3 June 2016.

However, deductions are subject to certain restrictions as prescribed by the anti-avoidance provisions of ss.17E, 17F and 17G.
