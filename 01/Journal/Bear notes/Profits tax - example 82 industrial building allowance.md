# Profits tax - example 82 industrial building allowance
Refer to the facts in Example 42 under Section 7.4 on 'Capital expenditure and revenue expenditure'. 

Required:

Analyse, with reference to the relevant tax principles, whether the Sum is allowable for profits tax deduction as an entitlement of capital allowance under the IRO.

Solution

Notwithstanding that the Sum was capital in nature, it does not follow that Gourmet is entitled to the deduction of the industrial building allowance or commercial building allowance in respect thereof.

S.34 provides that a person is entitled to the deduction of industrial building allowance when certain conditions are met. Where a person incurred capital expenditure on the construction of a building or structure which is an industrial building or structure and occupied it for the purposes of a trade, he is entitled to a deduction of initial allowance (s.34(1)). Where a person is entitled to an interest in a building or structure which is an industrial building or structure and where that interest is the relevant interest in relation to the capital expenditure incurred on the construction of that building or structure, a person is entitled to a deduction of annual allowance (s.34(2)). As to commercial building allowance, a provision similar to s.34(2) is set out in s.33A(1).

In the present case, it is patently clear that the Sum was not incurred on the construction of the factory premises. Hence, Gourmet is neither entitled to a deduction of industrial building allowance nor commercial building allowance.
