# ethic in business self-test 1
If a company follows all relevant legislation and rules that apply to it, does it guarantee that the company is acting ethically?
- - - -
Answer 1

Although it can be argued that adherence to applicable laws and regulations is an ethical duty, just because a company complies with these, it does not guarantee that it will be considered to be acting ethically. Ethics is generally concerned with ideas of fairness and undertaking the ‘right’ course of action. Laws and other regulation can be considered to be a codification of this ‘right’ action, however there may be circumstances where there is no relevant rule or law and a company still has to determine an ethical course of action.

For example in areas of new technology, laws may lag behind the ethical issues that arise from innovation. This is a situation that internet service providers have found themselves in when determining the limits of appropriate uses to which data about their customer’s browsing habits could be put to.

Additionally actions that adhere to law and regulation may still be considered unethical if they fail to meet society’s expectations of appropriate conduct. For example, in some countries multi-national companies have received criticism for their exploitation of mismatches and ‘loopholes’ in international tax rules in order to minimise the taxes they pay. This is not an illegal activity, yet many would nonetheless consider it unethical behaviour.

Ultimately what is ethical is not a fixed objective standard, it varies between countries and over time. What is considered ethical is determined by society, so companies must take care to conform to local ethical standards (if they are to avoid the negative impact on their reputation that would be the likely result of perceived unethical behaviour).
