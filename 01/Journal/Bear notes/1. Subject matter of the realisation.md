# 1. Subject matter of the realisation
An asset may be purchased for personal enjoyment, production of income or trading. If neither personal enjoyment nor income could be obtained from the asset, the asset is likely to be acquired for trading purposes.

In Rutledge v CIR [(1929) 14 TC 490] a money lender acquired one million rolls of toilet paper and sold them at a profit. Having regard to the nature and quantity of the subject matter, the judge was of the view that the toilet paper had been acquired for trading as such large quantity of toilet paper could not be acquired for personal enjoyment or production of income.
