# FE Dec 19 Paper II Case
**CASE**
Wood Furniture Holdings Limited (“WFHL” or the “Company”) is incorporated in Hong Kong and is listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. WFHL and its subsidiaries (the “Group”) are controlled and 70% owned by the Wood Family. 

The Group has been engaging in selling different types of ready-made **furniture**, such as dining and coffee tables, chairs and benches, sofas and armchairs and sofa beds, etc. for more than ten years; and the Wood Collection Series are the signature and best-selling products of the Company in the past two years. 

The Group aims at producing and selling the best quality products to its local and overseas customers. The Group has an annual financial reporting date of 31 December and the financial statements are prepared in accordance with the Hong Kong Financial Reporting Standards (“HKFRSs”). 
Mr Thomas Wood is the founder of the Group. He is the Chairman of the Board and Executive Director of the Group. Thomas is in-charge-of the strategic direction and overall performance of the Group. The Board also consists of three other Independent Non-Executive Directors, Ms Amy Lee, Mr Peter Tse and Ms Mary Hung. Mr Richard Wood, the son of Thomas Wood, is the Head of the Manufacturing Team. The management team of the Group is not proactive in reacting to the news and providing the market with timely information. 

**Current operation**
Cozy Home (HK) Limited (“CHL”) is the wholly-owned subsidiary of the Group engaged in the trading of ready-made furniture in Hong Kong. CHL has two store locations and one warehouse in Hong Kong; and two warehouses outside Hong Kong (one in mainland China and one in Indonesia). The flagship store is located in Causeway Bay, one of Hong Kong’s premier shopping destinations for furniture; whereas another store is located in Shatin. Both **stores** are **leased** premises. The **self-owned** **warehouse** is located in Fo Tan and is close to the Shatin store. 
CHL provides a two-year warranty service to customers free-of-charge upon the purchase of any furniture items, and defective products can only be exchanged for the same or similar items within 30 days after delivery. 

**Co-operation with Globalmind Asia Limited**
CHL is currently under discussion with Globalmind Asia Limited (“GAL”), a corporate customer to sell 2,000 dining tables with the following three proposals: 
Proposal X – 2,000 dining tables are sold at a total consideration of HK$10,000,000 and the customers can return any dining tables and get a full refund within 180 days after the items are shipped to GAL and the return is subject to a cap of 10% (i.e. 200 tables). 

Proposal Y – The proposal is similar to that of Proposal X except the consideration is increased to HK$10,500,000 and the return is subject to a cap of 30% (i.e. 600 tables). 
Proposal Z – The proposal is similar to that of Proposal X except the consideration is decreased to HK$9,800,000 and the customers are required to pay a restocking fee of HK$1,500 to CHL for each dining table returned. 

Since the Group does not have a previous trading relationship with GAL, the Group would request settlement from GAL upon delivery. The Group expected the products return rate would be 10%, and it would incur refinishing and repackaging costs of HK$600 for each returned item. The inventories cost of the 2,000 dining tables amounted to HK$6,000,000. 

**Business restructuring**
The Group has been the leader in the Hong Kong market; thus, shareholders expected stable dividend payments. However, the Group did not maintain a stable dividend payout policy, which disappointed the shareholders. In the past two years, a number of small to medium sized furniture companies have set up their businesses in Hong Kong selling similar products with a much lower margin. Due to intense competition in the local market and rising rental in Hong Kong, Thomas decided to undergo business restructuring to secure the sustainability of the Group. 
During the year, the Group’s warehouse in Fo Tan (“Fo Tan Premise”) was closed as part of the business restructuring. To fund the business restructuring, Thomas is considering to dispose of the Fo Tan Premise, which was acquired by CHL in 2001 for its furniture trading business. Peony Investment (Hong Kong) Limited (“PIHKL”), a company owned by a good business friend of Thomas, would like to acquire the Fo Tan Premise from CHL. 
The negotiation with PIHKL in relation to the disposal of the Fo Tan Premise concluded after year end on 1 February 2019 at a consideration of HK$16.5 million. Notwithstanding the then fair value of the Fo Tan Premise on 1 February 2019 of HK$15 million, PIHKL is willing to pay a higher price to CHL since CHL would enter into another contract with PIHKL for the right to use the Fo Tan Premise for five years commencing 1 February 2019, with monthly rental payment of HK$60,000 payable at the end of each month, which is significantly higher than the market rental. The Fo Tan Premise leased from PIHKL would be used as the showroom of CHL. 
The present value of the monthly rental under the five-year lease period is HK$3.5 million and the then carrying value of the Fo Tan Premise is HK$9 million as at 1 February 2019. 
The legal title and the control of Fo Tan Premise, were transferred from CHL to PIHKL upon the lease commencement date on 1 February 2019. 
Furthermore, in late December 2018, CHL also closed its Shatin store and modified the store in Causeway Bay as a showroom. 

As a result, 80% of the employees were rendered redundant by the business restructuring. In addition to the entitlements as stipulated in the relevant employment contracts with the dismissed employees, CHL is required under the Employment Ordinance (“EO”) to make severance payment to each of them. Thomas anticipated that the dismissed employees would have difficulties in finding new jobs, as most of them are over 50 years of age with relatively low skills. To relieve their hardship, the severance payments accrued by CHL were in excess of the statutory requirements under the EO. The excess amount was equivalent to each of their three months’ salary. 
In the medium run, in order to increase the profit margin, Thomas plans to develop a website and launch an online platform by December 2022 so that customers can order the custom-made furniture online; and the Group expects the online sales will contribute more than 15% to the Group’s consolidated revenue. In the long run, in order to increase the profit margin, customers can place their orders through the online platform only. 
**New investment plan**
Currently, the Management is considering two new investment projects: Project A and Project B. It is expected that the Projects may generate a reasonable return to the Group in near future; however, each project requires HK$30 million as the initial investment. 
**Other matters**
In the past two years, in order to reduce material cost, the Group cut and consumed lots of woods in Indonesia for the production of Wood Series Collection furniture even though the Management noticed that the quality of the wood was bad. Several environmental groups and media expressed their concerns about the environmental issues and the Corporate Social Responsibilities (“CSR”) policies of the Group; and customers are dissatisfied with the product quality. The Group has been facing lots of pressure about the environmental issues and had received several customer complaints on the poor quality of the wooden furniture sold leading to some litigation issues. 
While reviewing some old files, Thomas discovered a tax receipt issued by the Indonesia tax authority, which acknowledged that the Company paid a royalty withholding tax of IDR92 million (equivalent to HK$50,000) in February 2017. The royalty withholding tax was paid with respect to the licensing of the Group’s trademark to its wholly-owned subsidiary in Indonesia during the year ended 31 December 2016. The subsidiary was closed in mid-2017. The royalty received was returned as taxable income in the Company’s 2016/17 profits tax return. However, no foreign tax credit claim was made in respect of the withholding tax paid in Indonesia in that profits tax return. Tax assessment for the year of assessment 2016/17 was issued as per the profits tax return submitted by CHL and no objection was lodged against the assessment. 
At the end of each year, Mr Dominic Lee, Head of Sales Department, updates the sales performance to Mr Paul Lee, Finance Director of the Group, so that Paul can update the Management about the sales performance.

Auditor of the Group
YL & Co is the auditor of the Group since year 2015. During the audit for the year ended 31 December 2018, Thomas contacted Peter Kwok, audit manager of the engagement for YL & Co, on a proposal intending to reduce the audit fee for the current year. Thomas explained that due to the poor results of the Group, the plan for the closure of the warehouse and lay-off of staff; the Group would like to keep more cash on hand, and thus, proposed for a revised audit fee arrangement as below: 
* (i)  YL & Co to reduce the up-front audit fee for the current year for 30% (approximately HK$600,000); 

* (ii)  Thomas is offering to the audit partner and Peter any wooden furniture they have in the warehouse up to a total cost of HK$800,000, which also helps to reduce their stock level; 

* (iii)  Depending on the audited results of the group for the current year, if the audited loss of the Group is less than HK$8,500,000, Thomas would be willing to pay an additional audit fee of HK$200,000 to YL & Co. 

During the audit, Peter also noted that the newly employed inventory warehouse manager of the Group was granted “super user” access to all the various operational and accounting systems of the Group. 
In an update meeting with the audit partner, the audit manager has concluded with the audit partner the following matters to be communicated as the key audit matters for the Group for the current year: 
* Working capital sufficiency 

* Revenue recognition – cutoff (also considered as a significant risk during the audit) 

* Provision for closure of a warehouse in Fo Tan and a store in Shatin (also considered 
as a significant risk during the audit) 

* Existence of inventory
