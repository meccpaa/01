# FI Example: Rebalancing
At 1 June 20X5, Watson Brown Company (WBC) expects to purchase 1 million barrels of West Texas crude oil in 12 months' time. WBC designates a futures contract of 1.05 million barrels of Brent crude oil in a cash flow hedge to hedge this highly probable forecast purchase. The hedge ratio is 1.05:1.

At 31 December 20X5, the cumulative change in the fair value of the hedged item is $190,000 and the cumulative change in the fair value of the hedging instrument is $205,000.

WBC's treasurer rebalances the hedging relationship and resets the hedge ratio to 0.98:1. This is achieved by de-designating part of the hedging instrument.

What accounting entries are required at 31 December 20X5?
- - - -
**Solution**
The hedging relationship at 31 December 20X5 is accounted for by 

DEBIT	OCI (effective hedge)		$190K
DEBIT	P/L (ineffective hedge)	$15K
CREDIT	Derivatives - hedging intr	$205K

To record the inception of the loan.
To account for the fair value change in the hedging instrument.

The hedge ratio is rebalanced to 0.98:1. Therefore of the 1.05 million barrels of West Texas crude oil that formed part of the original hedge, only 980,000 barrels will remain a designated hedging instrument. The remaining 70,000 will be de-designated and hedge accounting will not apply.

The part of the futures contract that is no longer a designated hedging item is therefore transferred to be a held for trading derivative by:

DEBIT	Derivatives - hedging intr	$13,667 = (7/105 x 205K)
CREDIT	Derivatives - held for trading $13,667
To account for the fact that part of the forward contract is no longer part of a hedging relationship.
