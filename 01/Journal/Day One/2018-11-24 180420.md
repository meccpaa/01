# Revenue - 5 Step approach - exam (2018 Jun)

Fantastic Music (H.K.) Company Limited

Fantastic Music (H.K.) Company Limited (“Fantastic”) is a subsidiary of Music Land. Students can join a one-hour class without any commitment and pay each time at a fee of HK$800. Fantastic has over 150 instructors and they are either full-time or part-time employees of Fantastic. An additional 20% of the fee will be charged if a specific instructor is selected. Fantastic shares the fee with instructors on a negotiated basis.

In addition, Fantastic offers different kinds of courses. It offers single 10-lesson and 20-lesson one-hour private class courses for playing different musical instruments in Music Land. The course fees of the 10-lesson course and 20-lesson course are HK$6,000 and HK$12,000 respectively, which include the tuition fee of the instructors, rental services of the music room and / or the musical instruments (if necessary) to be used in the class. The course fee should be fully paid in advance before the commencement of the first lesson and it is non-refundable under any circumstance unless Fantastic fails to perform. The 10-lesson course must be completed within three months, with a maximum of one lesson per week. The 20-lesson course must be completed within six months, with a maximum of two lessons per week. As a kind of incentive, students can enjoy one extra lesson free-of-charge if they join the 20-lesson course.

In addition, based on the customary business practices of the Group, 10% and 15% discount coupons are given for students who join the 10-lesson and 20-lesson courses, respectively, to purchase musical instruments from the Group and the coupon is effective one month from the date of joining the course. Normally the Group would only give a 5% discount to students who do not join a course. Based on Fantastic’s experience, 80% of the students will redeem the coupons before expiry.

Question 2 (13 marks – approximately 23 minutes)

Assume that you are Rita Liu, the financial controller of Grand Music Group. Write a memo to Mr Julian Wong to advise as to the accounting treatment for revenue recognition in relation to the 20-lesson course offered by Fantastic using the five-step model under HKFRS 15 Revenue from Contracts with Customers.

Accounting for revenue recognition in relation to the 20-lesson course

Step 1: Identify the contract with a customer

A contract exists on the date of contract inception as:









The contract is approved by Fantastic and the customer upon the date of contract inception and they are committed to perform the relevant performance obligations.

The contract has commercial substance – Fantastic’s cash flows change as a result of this contract, i.e. it receives HK$12,000 from the customer.

The right of Fantastic and the customer regarding the promised goods and services can be identified – 20 lessons as a part of the course plus one extra free lesson and implied promise regarding discount coupon for purchasing musical instruments at 15% off.

There are identifiable payment terms for the promised goods or services HK$12,000 is payable immediately.

It is probable that Fantastic will collect the consideration to which it is entitled – the consideration of HK$12,000 is received in full upon entering into the contract.

Step 2: Identify the performance obligations in the contract

Fantastic will identify, at contract inception, the goods or services that are promised to the customer in the contract, which includes both explicit promise (20 lessons and 1 free lesson) plus the implied promise based on the customary business practices (the 15% off discount in purchasing the musical instruments) as those promises create a valid expectation of the customer that Fantastic will transfer a good or service to the customer.

In relation to the 15% off discount coupon offered to the customer in purchasing the musical instruments, this option would be considered as a material right to the customer (hence separate performance obligation) as it exceeded the 5% discount offered to other customers without entering into the contracts.

In accordance with para.27 of HKFRS 15, a good or service is distinct if both of the criteria are met:





The customer can benefit from the good or service either on its own or together with other resources that are readily available to the customer (i.e. the good or service is capable of being distinct).

The promise to transfer the good or service to the customer is separately identifiable from any other promise in the contract (i.e. the promise to transfer the good or service is distinct within the context of the contract).

Fantastic offered the classes to participants separately and it demonstrated that each class provides benefit to students on its own and hence each individual class is capable of being distinct.

The factors as stated in para.29 of HKFRS 15 should be considered to determine whether the classes are inputs to a combined item.







Fantastic does not provide a significant integration service as it is able to fulfil each class on a separate basis.

Each class does not significantly modify or customise one or more of the other classes.

Each class is not highly interdependent or highly interrelated as Fantastic would be able to fulfil each class independently.

Therefore, depending on facts and circumstances, the 21 classes tend to be 21 separate performance obligations unless one may argue the course is structured in a way that the course is significantly customised or integrated so that the individual class inputs create a total learning experience to tailor to the students’ learning needs / requirements.

Furthermore, pursuant to para.22 to HKFRS 15, the 21 classes would be bundled as one performance obligation as the individual music classes are a series of distinct services that are substantially the same and have the same pattern of transfer (the performance obligation is satisfied over time – discussed in step 5 below and same method would be used to measure the entity’s progress towards complete satisfaction of the performance obligation to transfer each distinct good or service in the series (output method – time-based measure) to the customer. On that basis, it makes no difference as the overall result for the music classes would be bundled as one performance obligation.

Step 3: Determine the transaction price

The total consideration of HK$12,000 would be the transaction price. When the customer enrols for the 20-lesson course, HK$12,000 would be immediately payable. However, no significant financing components require adjustment over the transaction price which is required as the courses will be finished in six months (within one year).

Step 4: Allocate the transaction price to performance obligations in the contract

The total consideration of HK$12,000 would be allocated to the courses and the discount coupon on a relative standalone selling price basis.

The standalone selling price for the classes is directly observable. However, in relation to the discount coupon, there is no directly observable standalone selling price and hence an estimation is needed.

Fantastic may estimate the value of the discount coupon that the customer would obtain when exercising the option (15% x selling price of the musical instruments purchased by the customer) and adjusted by any discount that the customer could receive without exercising the option (5% x selling price of the musical instruments purchased by the customer) and the likelihood that the option will be exercised (80%).

Step 5: Recognise revenue when (or as) the entity satisfies a performance obligation

Fantastic should recognize revenue for classes over time for the classes as the customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefits provided as Fantastic performs. An output method (time-based measure) would be the revenue recognition method that most faithfully depicts Fantastic’s performance in transferring control of the services to the customer.

Fantastic recognises revenue for the discount coupon when the customer redeems it for purchasing the musical instruments at a discount or when it expires.
