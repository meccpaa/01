The Ranks (from highest to lowest)

· Ace (often abbreviated A)
· King (often abbreviated K)
· Queen (often abbreviated Q)
· Jack (often abbreviated J)
· 10 (Ten)

The cards from 2 through 10 are often called "pip cards" or "number cards." The Jack, Queen, and King are called "face cards" or "court cards."
***
The Suits

A standard deck has four suits:

· Hearts (♥)
· Diamonds (♦)
· Clubs (♣)
· Spades (♠)
***
Full Card Names

To name a specific card, you combine the rank with the suit. For example:

· Ace of Spades
· King of Hearts
· Queen of Diamonds
· Jack of Clubs
· 10 of Hearts
· 2 of Clubs

Special Notes:

· Ace: Can often be used as a low card (below 2) or a high card (above King), depending on the game.
· Jack: Was historically called the Knave.
· Joker: While not part of the standard 52-card ranking, many decks include one or two Jokers as special wild cards.

Collective Names for Groups of Cards:

· A pair = Two cards of the same rank (e.g., two Kings).
· Three of a kind = Three cards of the same rank.
· A flush = Five cards of the same suit.
· A full house = Three of a kind and a pair.
